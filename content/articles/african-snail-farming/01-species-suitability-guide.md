# Species Suitability Guide: Choosing the Right African Land Snail for Your Region

> **Part 1 of the series:** *[Snail Farming Across African Regions](./00-series-hub.md)*

**Key takeaways**

- ***Archachatina marginata*** is the most forgiving and widely recommended species for commercial farming in West and Central Africa. It lays few eggs, but its hatchlings are large and hardy.
- ***Achatina achatina*** (the "tiger snail") grows to a very large size and fetches premium prices, but it needs **consistently high humidity**. It does poorly wherever there is a pronounced dry season.
- ***Lissachatina fulica*** (formerly *Achatina fulica*) is the fastest breeder, but it is a **globally listed invasive pest**, sells for less in West African markets and is illegal to move in many jurisdictions.
- **Match the species to your climate first and your market second.** A premium species that spends five months a year in aestivation is less profitable than a hardy species that feeds all year.

---

## Why Species Choice Decides Success

Every species of giant African land snail evolved in a particular habitat. *A. achatina* evolved under the closed canopy of the West African rainforest, where the air is close to saturated for most of the year. *L. fulica* evolved in the seasonally dry coastal scrub and forest edges of East Africa, so it copes with heat and drought better. When farmers ignore these origins, for example by stocking tiger snails in the Guinea savanna, the snails spend the dry season dormant and the farm runs at a loss.

## Species Profiles

### 1. *Archachatina marginata*: the "big black snail"

| Attribute | Typical range (reported in extension literature) |
|-----------|---------------------------------------------------|
| Native range | Rainforest and forest-savanna mosaic from southern Nigeria through Cameroon, Gabon and the Congo Basin. Also found in parts of Ghana and neighbouring states. |
| Adult shell length | 12–20 cm |
| Live weight (market adult) | 150–500 g, depending on age and feeding |
| Clutch size | Small: about 4–18 large eggs |
| Clutches per year | Several, concentrated in the rainy season |
| Incubation | About 4–6 weeks at 25–28 °C |
| Age at sexual maturity | About 12–18 months |
| Lifespan | 5–10 years in captivity |
| Climate tolerance | Moderate. It tolerates short dry spells better than *A. achatina*. |

**Why farmers choose it**

- Its hatchlings are large and robust, and survive much better than those of *A. achatina*.
- It is docile, easy to handle and does well at farm stocking densities.
- Consumers know it and accept it everywhere in southern Nigeria, Cameroon and Gabon.
- Pigmented varieties (dark flesh) and the pale-fleshed *ovum* form both sell well. Some buyers prefer the pale meat.

**Limitations**

- Its low egg output means slow herd expansion. A farm must keep a **large breeder stock** to reach volume.
- Growth to market size usually takes 12–24 months, which ties up working capital.

**Best regions:** Southern Nigeria, southern Ghana, southern and littoral Cameroon, Gabon, Equatorial Guinea and the Republic of Congo. In East and Southern Africa it is **non-native**, so check the import and alien-species rules first.

---

### 2. *Achatina achatina*: the giant tiger snail

| Attribute | Typical range |
|-----------|---------------|
| Native range | West African rainforest belt: Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin and southern Nigeria |
| Adult shell length | 15–25 cm (exceptional specimens are larger) |
| Live weight (market adult) | 250–600 g+ |
| Clutch size | Large: commonly reported at 100–400 small eggs |
| Clutches per year | 1–3, strongly tied to the rainy season |
| Incubation | About 3–5 weeks |
| Age at sexual maturity | About 18–24 months |
| Climate tolerance | **Low.** It needs humidity above about 80 % to feed and breed well. |

**Why farmers choose it**

- It is the **largest and highest-value** snail in Ghanaian and Ivorian markets.
- A single successful clutch can produce hundreds of hatchlings.

**Limitations**

- Hatchlings are small and fragile, and often die in large numbers when humidity falls.
- It aestivates readily and for long periods in the dry season, sometimes for months.
- It grows slowly, and young snails often fail to thrive at high stocking densities.
- It is **not recommended for beginners** or for any site with a dry season longer than about three months, unless the pens are climate-controlled.

**Best regions:** The humid forest of Ghana (Ashanti, Western, Eastern and Central regions), southern Côte d'Ivoire, Liberia, and the wettest parts of southern Nigeria (Cross River, Akwa Ibom, Rivers, Bayelsa).

---

### 3. *Lissachatina fulica* (syn. *Achatina fulica*): the East African land snail

| Attribute | Typical range |
|-----------|---------------|
| Native range | Coastal East Africa (Kenya and Tanzania) |
| Adult shell length | 7–15 cm |
| Live weight | 50–150 g |
| Clutch size | 100–400 eggs |
| Clutches per year | Many; up to about 1,000+ eggs a year has been reported |
| Age at sexual maturity | About 5–8 months |
| Climate tolerance | **High.** It survives drought by aestivating for long periods and tolerates cooler nights than the West African species. |

**Why it attracts farmers**

- It reproduces extremely fast and reaches market size quickly.
- It is hardy in marginal climates.

**Serious cautions**

- It is listed among the world's most damaging **invasive species**. It eats crops and ornamental plants and can carry parasites, including the rat lungworm (*Angiostrongylus cantonensis*), which can infect people who eat it undercooked.
- Many countries **prohibit** importing, keeping or moving it. Outside its native range it may be illegal to farm.
- In West African markets it is smaller and less preferred, so its price per snail is much lower than for *A. marginata* or *A. achatina*.

**Best regions:** It is native to and permitted for farming only within parts of **coastal East Africa**, subject to wildlife permits. **Do not introduce it** anywhere it is not already established.

---

### 4. *Lissachatina immaculata* (syn. *Achatina immaculata*): the Southern African giant snail

- **Native range:** The subtropical eastern parts of Southern Africa, including KwaZulu-Natal, Mpumalanga, Limpopo, Eswatini and Mozambique, with related forms further north.
- **Size:** A large species, comparable to *A. marginata*.
- **Climate tolerance:** It is adapted to **summer rainfall with a dry winter**. It tolerates cooler winters by hibernating.
- **Farming status:** It is not widely farmed commercially. It is the **logical native candidate** for GALS-type farming in South Africa's subtropical east, because using a native species avoids alien-species permits. Breeding data are limited, so pilot first.

---

### 5. *Cornu aspersum* (syn. *Helix aspersa*): the European "petit-gris"

This is not a giant African land snail. It is included because it is the **main commercially farmed snail in South Africa's Western Cape** and in parts of North Africa.

- **Origin:** The Mediterranean and Western Europe. It is introduced and naturalised in South Africa.
- **Climate fit:** **Mediterranean climates** with cool, wet winters and moderate summers. It prefers 15–22 °C, so it suits places that are **too cold for GALS**.
- **Market:** The restaurant escargot trade, export of prepared escargot, and snail mucin for cosmetics.
- **Caution:** It is an agricultural pest in vineyards and orchards. Check provincial regulations before farming or moving stock.

## Decision Guide: Which Species Should You Farm?

1. **Is the species native to your area, or legally permitted there?**
   If not, stop. Choose a native species or obtain the necessary permits before going further.
2. **How long is your dry season, defined as months with RH regularly below 70 %?**
   - 0–2 months: any of the West African species will work. *A. achatina* is viable.
   - 3–4 months: choose *A. marginata*. Plan irrigation and shading.
   - More than 4 months: GALS farming needs a climate-controlled house (see [Mitigation Systems](./09-mitigation-systems.md)) or a cooler-climate species.
3. **Do night-time temperatures fall below 15 °C for more than a few weeks?**
   If yes, avoid *A. achatina* and *A. marginata* unless the pens are insulated or heated. Consider *C. aspersum* in Mediterranean climates.
4. **What does your market want?**
   West African buyers pay a premium for size, so *A. marginata* and *A. achatina* sell best. Cosmetic mucin processors care more about volume and mucus quality than shell size.

---

## Sourcing Healthy Breeding Stock

- **Buy from established farms, not from the wild.** Wild-collected snails may carry parasites, have been exposed to pesticides, or be older than they look.
- **Check the shell lip.** A thick, hard, turned-out lip (the peristome) means the snail is mature and has stopped growing. A thin, brittle lip means it is still growing.
- **Weigh breeders.** For *A. marginata*, breeders of about **100 g and above** are commonly recommended.
- **Quarantine all new stock for 2–4 weeks** in a separate pen, watching for slow feeding, discoloured mucus or broken shells.
- **Avoid "buy-back" investment schemes** that promise to buy your harvest at fixed prices. Several of these schemes across Africa have collapsed and left farmers with stock they could not sell.
