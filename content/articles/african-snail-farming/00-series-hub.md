# Snail Farming Across African Regions: The Complete Knowledge Base

Africa is the original home of the giant African land snails (GALS), the largest land snails farmed anywhere in the world. From the rainforest belt of southern Nigeria to the Lake Victoria basin and the subtropical coast of KwaZulu-Natal, snail farming (heliciculture) offers smallholders and investors a low-land-use source of protein with a strong market. It also fails often, nearly always for the same reason: **the snails are kept in conditions their biology cannot tolerate.**

This series sets out the operating conditions, species choices and market dynamics of Africa's main snail-farming hubs. It also gives clear, testable guidance on **where giant African land snails will not thrive** and what can be done about it.

> **How to use this series:** If you already have a site, start with the [Site Suitability Diagnostic Checklist](./08-site-suitability-diagnostic-checklist.md). If you are still choosing a region or species, start with the [Species Suitability Guide](./01-species-suitability-guide.md) and then read the guide for your region.

## The Five Numbers Every African Snail Farmer Must Know

Giant African land snails will tolerate a lot, but they **thrive and breed profitably** only inside a fairly narrow set of conditions. These five ranges come up again and again in the series:

| Parameter | Optimal range | Stress zone | Failure zone |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|--------------|
| Air temperature | 22–30 °C | 15–21 °C or 31–34 °C | Below ~12 °C for long periods, any frost, or above ~35 °C |
| Relative humidity (RH) | 80–95 % | 65–79 % | Below ~60 % for long periods |
| Soil moisture | Damp and crumbly. Holds together when squeezed but releases no water. | Dusty surface crust or a sticky film | Bone dry or waterlogged |
| Soil pH | 6.5–7.5 | 5.5–6.4 or 7.6–8.0 | Below ~5.5 or above ~8.5 |
| Light | Shade; about 12 hours of natural day length | Direct morning sun only | Full sun on the pens |

When conditions drift into the stress zone, snails eat less, grow more slowly and lay fewer eggs. In the failure zone they withdraw into their shells and seal the opening with a dried mucus membrane (the **epiphragm**), entering a dormant state. In the heat and dryness this is called **aestivation**; in the cold it is called **hibernation**. A dormant snail is not growing and not breeding, so on a farm, a dormant snail is a cost.

## Regulatory Note for the Whole Series

Several African countries treat land snails as **wildlife** or regulate them as **invasive or alien species**, particularly outside their native range. Before you buy, move or import breeding stock:

1. Confirm whether the species is **native** to your area.
2. Check whether you need a **wildlife farming, game ranching or alien-species permit** from your national or provincial authority.
3. Never release snails, or dump farm waste containing eggs, into the wild.

Each regional article flags the authorities to contact. Laws change, so always confirm the current position with the authority directly before acting.

## Editorial Standards

This series follows the Snail World editorial standard. Operational figures (temperatures, densities, clutch sizes, growth periods) are drawn from published heliciculture extension literature and are given as **ranges**, because results vary with strain, feed and husbandry. Prices are described in terms of how they behave over the year rather than as fixed figures, because currency movements in Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya and other markets quickly make quoted prices out of date. Check local market prices before building a business plan.
