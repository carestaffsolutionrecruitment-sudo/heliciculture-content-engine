# Southern Africa: Snail Farming in South Africa and Neighbouring States

> **Part 6 of the series:** *[Snail Farming Across African Regions](./00-series-hub.md)*

**Key takeaways**

- South Africa effectively has **two separate snail industries**:
  1. **European-style escargot farming** (mainly *Cornu aspersum*) in the **Western Cape** and other temperate areas. It serves restaurants, exports and the cosmetics trade.
  2. **Giant African land snail (GALS) farming**. It is limited to the **frost-free subtropical east** (the KwaZulu-Natal coast and the Mpumalanga and Limpopo lowveld) and is regulated because of alien-species law.
- The **Highveld** (Gauteng, the Free State, much of North West and Mpumalanga) gets **winter frost** and has a long dry season. It is unsuitable for outdoor GALS farming, even though Gauteng has the largest market of GALS-eating diaspora consumers.
- **The arid west** (Northern Cape, the Karoo, Namibia and Botswana) is not viable for any outdoor snail farming.
- **Alien and invasive species legislation (NEM:BA)** and provincial conservation rules apply. Get permits **before** buying or importing any snail species.

## 1. South Africa's Climate Zones and Snail Suitability

| Zone | Areas | Climate | GALS suitability | *Cornu aspersum* (escargot) suitability |
|------|-------|---------|------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| **Winter-rainfall (Mediterranean)** | Western Cape: Cape Town, Stellenbosch, Paarl, Overberg, Boland | Cool wet winters; warm dry summers | ☆☆☆☆☆ Too cool in winter, too dry in summer | ★★★★☆ **South Africa's escargot heartland**, with summer irrigation and shading |
| **All-year rainfall (southern coast)** | Garden Route (George, Knysna), coastal Eastern Cape | Mild and moderately humid all year | ★☆☆☆☆ Too cool | ★★★★☆ Good |
| **Subtropical humid east coast** | KwaZulu-Natal coast (Durban, North and South Coast, Zululand coast), north-east Eastern Cape coast | Hot humid summers; mild frost-free winters | ★★★☆☆ **The best GALS zone in South Africa.** Winter slowdown is likely. | ★★☆☆☆ Too hot in summer |
| **Lowveld** | Mpumalanga lowveld (Mbombela, Barberton, Hazyview), Limpopo lowveld (Tzaneen, Phalaborwa area) | Hot wet summers; warm dry winters, mostly frost-free | ★★★☆☆ Good in summer; needs dry-winter irrigation | ★☆☆☆☆ Too hot |
| **Highveld** | Gauteng, Free State, North West, Mpumalanga Highveld | Summer rain; **cold, dry, frosty winters** (often below 0 °C) | ☆☆☆☆☆ **Unsuitable outdoors** | ★★☆☆☆ Possible with winter housing |
| **Arid and semi-arid west** | Northern Cape, the Karoo, western North West | Very low rainfall; extreme temperatures | ☆☆☆☆☆ Not viable | ☆☆☆☆☆ Not viable |

## 2. The Escargot Industry: *Cornu aspersum* in the Western Cape

The Western Cape's cool, wet winters and moderate temperatures resemble southern Europe's, which makes it suitable for the **petit-gris** (*Cornu aspersum*, formerly *Helix aspersa*). This snail was introduced to South Africa long ago and is now naturalised, and is also a garden and vineyard pest.

### Operating conditions

| Parameter | Recommended practice |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Active season | Autumn to spring (about April to October), during the rains |
| Summer management | Shade netting and **sprinkler or mist irrigation** in the evenings. The dry Cape summer otherwise forces aestivation. |
| Systems | Outdoor **"Italian-style" open-field parks**: netted plots of cover crops (clover, radish, chard) with mesh perimeter fences. Some farms use mixed systems with indoor breeding. |
| Temperature target | 15–22 °C. Above about 28 °C activity falls sharply. |
| Hibernation | Snails slow down in the coldest weeks. Some farms use controlled hibernation to synchronise breeding. |

### Market

- **Fine-dining restaurants** and hotels, particularly in Cape Town, the Winelands and Johannesburg.
- **Competition from imported canned escargot.** Domestic producers compete on **freshness, provenance and local sourcing**.
- **Snail mucin (slime) for cosmetics**, an increasingly important outlet. Several South African mucin brands and contract-supply arrangements have emerged.
- **Export** of processed product, with strict sanitary certification.

## 3. Giant African Land Snails in South Africa

### Native giant snails

South Africa's subtropical east is home to native achatinid snails, most notably ***Lissachatina immaculata*** (syn. *Achatina immaculata*). Its natural range covers parts of KwaZulu-Natal, Mpumalanga, Limpopo and neighbouring Eswatini and Mozambique.

- **Advantage:** A native species avoids most alien-species complications (subject to provincial rules on collecting and keeping indigenous fauna).
- **Challenge:** There is very little published commercial husbandry data. Run a **pilot breeding programme** of at least one full year before investing at scale.

### Non-native giant snails

- ***Archachatina marginata*** and ***Achatina achatina*** are **not native** to South Africa. Importing and keeping them is likely to require permits under alien-species legislation, if it is allowed at all.
- ***Lissachatina fulica*** is a **globally recognised invasive pest**. Introducing it to new areas is a serious biosecurity risk.

### The Gauteng demand problem

Johannesburg and Pretoria have large West and Central African diaspora communities with strong demand for **large GALS** for traditional dishes. But the Highveld climate rules out outdoor GALS farming. The realistic options are:

1. **Fully enclosed, heated and humidified houses** in Gauteng. These are technically possible but energy-intensive. Solar and thermal-mass design is essential (see [Mitigation Systems](./09-mitigation-systems.md)).
2. **Production in KZN or the lowveld**, trucked to Gauteng.
3. **Legal imports** of processed snail meat. These need import permits and sanitary compliance.

## 4. Regulation: Alien-Species Law and Conservation Permits

> **Important:** The rules below are a general orientation, not legal advice. Confirm current requirements in writing with the authorities before buying, moving or importing any snail.

- **National Environmental Management: Biodiversity Act (NEM:BA)** and its **Alien and Invasive Species (AIS) Regulations and Lists** regulate the import, possession, breeding, sale and transport of listed alien species. Activities involving listed species may require a **permit from the Department of Forestry, Fisheries and the Environment (DFFE)**, and some species are prohibited outright.
- **Provincial conservation authorities** regulate collecting, keeping and trading in indigenous fauna. Examples include **CapeNature** (Western Cape), **Ezemvelo KZN Wildlife** (KwaZulu-Natal), the **Mpumalanga Tourism and Parks Agency** and Limpopo's environmental department.
- **Agricultural import controls** (the **Department of Agriculture**) apply to importing live animals and animal products.
- **Food safety:** Selling snail meat for human consumption must comply with food-safety regulations. Processing facilities may need a certificate of acceptability from the local municipal environmental health office.

**Practical sequence**

1. Identify your exact species (by scientific name).
2. Check whether it is indigenous to your province or on the NEM:BA AIS lists.
3. Contact the provincial conservation authority and, for alien species, DFFE.
4. Get permits for **possession, breeding, sale and transport** as needed.
5. Design escape-proof facilities. Inspectors are likely to check.

## 5. Neighbouring States at a Glance

| Country | Suitable zones | Limitations | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-------------|-------|
| **Mozambique** | Coastal lowlands (Maputo to Pemba), Zambezi valley, central coast (Beira, Quelimane) | Dry season inland (about May to October); cyclone risk on the coast | Native achatinids are present. Tropical humid coast is broadly suitable. |
| **Malawi** | Lake Malawi lakeshore, Lower Shire valley | Long dry season; cooler plateau | Lakeshore humidity helps. Highland areas (Zomba, Blantyre at about 1,000 m+) are cooler. |
| **Zambia** | Northern high-rainfall areas (Northern, Luapula, Copperbelt), Zambezi valley lowlands | **Long dry season** (about May to October); cool plateau winters | Marginal outdoors. Housed systems needed for production all year. |
| **Zimbabwe** | Eastern Highlands (lower slopes, Chimanimani), Zambezi valley lowlands | Plateau at about 1,200–1,500 m with **cool, dry winters and occasional frost** | Lowveld is hot but dry. Housed systems are needed on the plateau. |
| **Angola** | Northern humid forest and savanna (Cabinda, Uíge, Zaire provinces) | The south is arid | The north is ecologically closer to Central Africa and naturally suitable. |
| **Botswana and Namibia** | None outdoors | Arid and semi-arid | Not viable outside fully controlled indoor facilities |
| **Eswatini and Lesotho** | Eswatini lowveld (subtropical) | Lesotho is entirely high altitude and frosty | Eswatini's lowveld resembles Mpumalanga's. Lesotho is unsuitable. |

## 6. Operating Conditions: Summary for the Region

| Need | Western Cape (escargot) | KZN coast and lowveld (GALS) | Highveld (any species) |
|------|-------------------------|-------------------------------|------------------------|
| Summer management | Shade and irrigation | Shade, ventilation, watching for heat stress | Shade |
| Winter management | Allow natural hibernation or manage it | Slowdown is likely, so cover pens and add thermal mass | **Full enclosure and heating** |
| Soil | Usually neutral to alkaline; check | Coastal sands drain fast, so add organic matter. Some soils are acidic. | Check pH |
| Primary market | Restaurants, mucin, export | Diaspora (Gauteng, Durban), restaurants | Local diaspora, mucin |
