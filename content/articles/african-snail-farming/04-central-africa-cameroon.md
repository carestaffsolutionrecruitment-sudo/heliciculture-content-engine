# Central Africa: Snail Farming in Cameroon and the Congo Basin

> **Part 4 of the series:** *[Snail Farming Across African Regions](./00-series-hub.md)*

**Key takeaways**

- Cameroon is often called "Africa in miniature". Within one country it has some of the **wettest places on Earth**, cool **mountain plateaus** and **Sahelian semi-desert**, so suitability depends heavily on the site.
- The **Littoral, South-West, Centre, South and East regions** are excellent for *Archachatina marginata* and *Achatina achatina*.
- The **Western Highlands** (West and North-West regions) are cooler and need careful site selection or protected housing. The **North and Far North** are unsuitable outdoors.
- Central Africa's snail demand is closely linked to **bushmeat culture**. Conservation programmes often promote snail farming as a **legal, sustainable alternative to hunting**.

---

## 1. Cameroon's Agro-Ecological Zones

| Zone | Regions / key towns | Elevation | Annual rainfall (approx.) | Outdoor GALS suitability |
|------|---------------------|-----------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Monomodal humid forest (coastal)** | Littoral (Douala, Nkongsamba), South-West (Buea, Limbe, Kumba, Mamfe) | 0–1,000 m | 2,500–4,000 mm+ (much higher on the seaward slopes of Mount Cameroon) | ★★★★★ Excellent. Manage waterlogging and acidic soils. |
| **Bimodal humid forest (plateau)** | Centre (Yaoundé, Mbalmayo), South (Ebolowa, Kribi), East (Bertoua, Abong-Mbang) | 400–900 m | 1,500–2,000 mm | ★★★★★ Excellent. Two rainy seasons a year keep snails active most of the year. |
| **Western Highlands** | West (Bafoussam, Dschang, Foumban), North-West (Bamenda, Kumbo) | 1,000–2,500 m+ | 1,500–2,500 mm | ★★☆☆☆ to ★★★☆☆ Good rainfall, but **cool nights**. Suitability falls as altitude rises. |
| **High Guinea savanna (Adamawa Plateau)** | Adamawa (Ngaoundéré, Tibati) | 900–1,500 m | 1,400–1,600 mm | ★☆☆☆☆ Long dry season and cool nights |
| **Sudano-Sahelian** | North (Garoua), Far North (Maroua, Kousseri) | 200–500 m | 400–1,000 mm | ☆☆☆☆☆ **Not viable** outdoors |

### Coastal and southern forest: prime snail country

Around Buea, Limbe and Kumba, and through the forests south and east of Yaoundé, natural humidity rarely falls far below the GALS comfort zone. Snails remain active almost all year. The main challenges here are **too much water**, not too little:

- **Waterlogging:** Raise pens or build them on gentle slopes, and add drainage channels.
- **Fungal and bacterial problems:** Remove uneaten food every day, replace pen soil regularly and avoid stagnant pools.
- **Acidic, leached soils:** Very high rainfall washes calcium out of the soil. Test the pH and amend it with agricultural lime, wood ash or crushed shell.

### The Western Highlands: a special case

The highlands around Bamenda, Bafoussam and Dschang are among Cameroon's most productive farming areas, but snails face **cool night temperatures**, especially during the dry season (roughly November to February).

| Elevation band | Typical outcome | Recommendation |
|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Below ~1,200 m (valleys, lower slopes) | Snails active most of the year | *A. marginata* in shaded outdoor pens, with night insulation in the dry season |
| ~1,200–1,600 m | Slower growth; frequent dormancy on cold nights | **Covered or housed pens** with thermal mass (block walls, earthen floors). Bring nurseries indoors. |
| Above ~1,600 m | Snails regularly too cold to feed | Only in **fully housed, insulated systems**. Usually not economic. |

See [Climatic Limitations](./07-climatic-limitations.md) for the altitude rule of thumb and [Mitigation Systems](./09-mitigation-systems.md) for insulation designs.

## 2. Species Choice in Cameroon

| Species | Recommendation |
|---------|----------------|
| ***Archachatina marginata*** | **First choice** across the forest zones and lower highlands. It is native and well known in local markets. |
| ***Achatina achatina*** | Viable in the very wet coastal forest of the Littoral and South-West, and in the southern forest. Needs consistent humidity. |
| ***Lissachatina fulica*** | Reported in parts of the country. **Do not deliberately farm or spread it.** It is an invasive pest of crops and gardens and sells for less. |

## 3. Operating Conditions: The Cameroonian Baseline

| Parameter | Recommended practice |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Pen systems | Hutch boxes and trench pens for breeders and nurseries; fenced, net-roofed mini-paddocks under shade for growers |
| Shading | Plantain, banana, cocoa or *Gliricidia* canopy, plus a thatch or shade-net roof |
| Soil | Sieved forest loam with **pH checked and corrected**. Replace it every 3–6 months in high-rainfall zones. |
| Feed | Cocoyam leaves, pawpaw leaves and fruit, *Talinum* (waterleaf), cassava leaves (moderate), ripe plantain and banana, plus a concentrate with oyster shell or limestone |
| Pests | Soldier ants, rats, shrews, lizards, birds and theft |
| Wet-season hygiene | Clean daily, drain well, keep the soil from staying waterlogged |

## 4. Market Dynamics in Central Africa

### Domestic demand

- **Urban markets:** Douala and Yaoundé are the largest consumer markets. Buea, Limbe and Kumba are traditional hubs in the anglophone South-West.
- **Culinary use:** Snails are eaten grilled, in pepper soup, in groundnut and palm-nut sauces, and in stews with *ndolé* or *eru* (*okok*) in some households. Snail is valued as a delicacy and as a "meat of the forest".
- **Bushmeat linkage:** Snails are traditionally sold alongside other bushmeat. Wildlife law enforcement is reducing the hunting of protected mammals, so **farmed snails are positioned as a legal protein substitute**. Several conservation NGOs and community-forestry programmes in the Congo Basin have supported snail farming as an alternative livelihood.

### Cross-border trade

| Trade route | Notes |
|-------------|-------|
| **Cameroon → Nigeria** (via Mamfe–Ekok and the South-West–Cross River border) | Long-standing informal trade in live and smoked snails, driven by high Nigerian demand |
| **Cameroon → Gabon and Equatorial Guinea** (via Kyé-Ossi and Ambam) | Strong demand in Libreville, Malabo and Bata, where purchasing power is high. Prices are often higher than in Cameroon. |
| **Cameroon → Chad and the CAR** | Limited; mostly smoked or dried product |

Formal exporters must satisfy phytosanitary, veterinary and customs requirements in both countries. Informal trade is common but exposes traders to seizure and price risk.

### How prices move through the year

- **Wet season(s):** High wild supply, so lower prices.
- **Main dry season (about November to February in the south):** Wild supply falls and prices rise.
- In the **bimodal Centre and South regions**, the short dry season (about July to August) creates a **second, smaller price peak**.

## 5. The Wider Congo Basin

### Gabon and Equatorial Guinea

- They are almost entirely **humid equatorial forest**, which suits *A. marginata* and *A. achatina* very well.
- Their **high urban purchasing power** (Libreville, Port-Gentil, Malabo, Bata) and limited local farming mean demand is largely met by **imports from Cameroon** and by wild collection.
- This creates an opening for local producers who can deliver consistent volumes.

### Republic of the Congo and the Democratic Republic of the Congo

- The **central Congo Basin** has excellent humidity all year and is naturally suited to GALS.
- Logistics and cold-chain infrastructure are the main constraints, more than climate.
- Kinshasa and Brazzaville are significant urban markets for snails. Snails (*escargots*, or *nkoso* in some Lingala-speaking areas; local names vary) are widely eaten.
- The **eastern DRC highlands** (Kivu, around 1,500 m+) face the same altitude constraints as the Western Highlands of Cameroon.
- The **southern Katanga plateau** has a long dry season and cooler winters, so it is marginal.

### Central African Republic

- The **southern forest zone** (Lobaye, Sangha-Mbaéré) is suitable.
- The central and northern savannas have long dry seasons and are marginal to unsuitable outdoors.

---

## 6. Regulation and Support

- In Cameroon, **mini-livestock (*petit élevage*)** production, including snails, is generally within the remit of the **Ministry of Livestock, Fisheries and Animal Industries (MINEPIA)**. Collecting wild animals and trading in them may involve the **Ministry of Forestry and Wildlife (MINFOF)**. Check the current requirements with your regional delegation.
- Cross-border movement of live animals may need **veterinary certificates**. Requirements differ between CEMAC member states.
- University faculties of agronomy and agricultural research institutes in Cameroon have produced locally relevant research on *A. marginata* feeding and housing.
