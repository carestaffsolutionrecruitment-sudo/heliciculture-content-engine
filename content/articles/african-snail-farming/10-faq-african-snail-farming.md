# African Snail Farming FAQ: Regions, Species, Climate and Markets

> **Part 10 of the series:** *[Snail Farming Across African Regions](./00-series-hub.md)*

Quick answers to the questions we are asked most often. Each answer links to the full article for detail.

---

## Section 1: Regions and Suitability

### Which African countries are best for snail farming?
The most naturally suitable countries are those with humid lowland rainforest: southern Nigeria, southern Ghana, southern Côte d'Ivoire, Liberia, Sierra Leone, southern and coastal Cameroon, Gabon, Equatorial Guinea and the Congo Basin. Pockets of East Africa (the Kenyan coast and the Lake Victoria basin) and Southern Africa (the KwaZulu-Natal coast and the lowveld) are also suitable with good management. See the [Series Hub](./00-series-hub.md).

### Which Nigerian states are best for snail farming?
The lowland rainforest states: Edo, Delta, Ondo, Ogun, Ekiti, Osun, Cross River, Akwa Ibom, Rivers, Bayelsa, Imo, Abia and Anambra. Oyo, Enugu, Ebonyi and southern Kwara and Kogi are workable with shade and dry-season irrigation. See [Snail Farming in Nigeria](./02-west-africa-nigeria.md).

### Can I farm snails in northern Nigeria?
Not outdoors. The Sudan and Sahel savanna states have dry seasons of seven months or more and very low humidity during the Harmattan. Snail farming there is only possible in fully enclosed, humidified and cooled houses, and is usually less profitable than buying snails from the south.

### Can I farm snails in Abuja (FCT)?
Yes, but only with covered or housed systems. Abuja has a long dry season and a strong Harmattan, and outdoor pens usually lose months of production to aestivation. Covered pens or shaded polytunnels with misting are the minimum. See [Mitigation Systems](./09-mitigation-systems.md).

### Is Accra a good place to farm snails?
Accra is Ghana's largest market but sits on the dry Accra Plains, which form part of the Dahomey Gap. Most successful Accra-focused businesses either farm in the wetter Eastern or Ashanti regions and sell in Accra, or use humidified indoor housing in Accra. See [Snail Farming in Ghana](./03-west-africa-ghana.md).

### Can I farm snails in Nairobi?
Only in insulated, heated and humidified housing. At about 1,700 m, Nairobi's nights fall to around 10–12 °C in the cool season (June to August), which sends giant African land snails into dormancy. See [Snail Farming in Kenya and Uganda](./05-east-africa-kenya-uganda.md).

### Where is the best place to farm snails in Uganda?
The Lake Victoria crescent: Kampala, Wakiso, Mukono, Buikwe, Jinja and Masaka. It has two rainy seasons a year, high humidity and mild temperatures. The western highlands and Karamoja are unsuitable outdoors.

### Can I farm giant African land snails in Johannesburg?
Not outdoors. The Highveld gets frost and cold, dry winters. Only fully enclosed, heated and humidified houses can work there, and they are expensive to run. Production on the KwaZulu-Natal coast or in the lowveld, trucked to Gauteng, is usually more practical. See [Southern Africa](./06-southern-africa.md).

### Is the Western Cape suitable for snail farming?
Yes, for the European escargot species *Cornu aspersum*, which suits its Mediterranean climate. It is not suitable for giant African land snails, which find the winters too cool and the summers too dry.

### Which parts of Cameroon are best for snail farming?
The Littoral and South-West regions (Douala, Buea, Limbe, Kumba) and the southern forest plateau (Centre, South and East regions). The Western Highlands are suitable only at lower elevations or with protected housing. The North and Far North are unsuitable outdoors. See [Central Africa](./04-central-africa-cameroon.md).

## Section 2: Species

### What is the best snail species for beginners in Africa?
*Archachatina marginata*, in its native West and Central African range. It is hardy, its hatchlings survive well and buyers know it. See the [Species Suitability Guide](./01-species-suitability-guide.md).

### Is Achatina achatina better than Archachatina marginata?
*A. achatina* grows larger, fetches premium prices (especially in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire) and lays far more eggs. But it needs consistently high humidity, its hatchlings die more readily and it aestivates for long periods in the dry season. In most locations *A. marginata* is more reliably profitable.

### Should I farm Achatina fulica because it breeds faster?
Generally no. *Lissachatina fulica* (formerly *Achatina fulica*) is a globally recognised invasive pest, can carry the rat lungworm parasite, sells for less in West African markets and is prohibited in many jurisdictions. It is native only to coastal East Africa, and farming it there still requires wildlife permits and secure containment.

### What snail species can be farmed in South Africa?
The established commercial species is *Cornu aspersum* (escargot), mainly in the Western Cape. For giant snails, the native *Lissachatina immaculata* is the logical candidate in the subtropical east, but commercial data are limited. Non-native giant snails are likely to need alien-species permits, if they are allowed at all.

### How many eggs does Archachatina marginata lay?
Relatively few: commonly about 4–18 large eggs per clutch, several times a year, mostly in the rainy season. Its hatchlings are large and hardy, which partly makes up for the low egg count.

### How long do snails take to reach market size?
*A. marginata* generally takes about 12–24 months and *A. achatina* about 18–30 months, depending on feeding, climate and the target size. Long dry-season dormancy extends these times.

## Section 3: Climate, Humidity and Temperature

### What humidity do giant African land snails need?
Ideally 80–95 % relative humidity. Below about 70 % for long periods, feeding falls and juveniles struggle. Below about 60 %, most snails aestivate and hatchlings can die. See [Climatic Limitations](./07-climatic-limitations.md).

### What temperature is best for snail farming?
Roughly 22–30 °C. Below about 15 °C snails slow down sharply, and below about 10–12 °C they generally become dormant. Above about 35 °C they suffer heat stress and dehydration. Frost is usually fatal to tropical species.

### What is aestivation and why does it matter?
Aestivation is a dormant state snails enter when it is too hot or dry. They seal their shell with a dried mucus membrane (the epiphragm) and stop feeding, growing and breeding. On a farm, every week of aestivation is a week of costs with no growth.

### How does the Harmattan affect snail farms?
The Harmattan is a dry, dusty north-easterly wind that blows from about November to February. It sharply lowers humidity and increases day–night temperature swings across West Africa. Snails aestivate and young snails can die of dehydration. Windbreaks, double shading, mulch, evening watering and bringing nurseries indoors are the main defences. See [Snail Farming in Nigeria](./02-west-africa-nigeria.md).

### Does altitude affect snail farming?
Yes. Temperature falls by about 6–6.5 °C for every 1,000 m of elevation. In tropical Africa, sites below about 1,200 m are usually warm enough. Sites at 1,400–1,800 m are in the stress zone, and sites above about 1,800 m are unsuitable for outdoor giant-snail farming.

### Where in Africa will giant African land snails not thrive?
Deserts and the Sahel (from Mauritania to Sudan), the Horn of Africa, the Kalahari, the Namib and the Karoo; high-altitude areas such as the Ethiopian Highlands, the East African highlands, Rwanda, Burundi and Lesotho; the frosty Southern African Highveld; the Mediterranean-climate Maghreb and Western Cape (too cool for giant species); and any site with a long dry season, very acidic or contaminated soil, or exposed, sun-baked conditions.

### How do I measure humidity on my farm?
Place a digital thermo-hygrometer, ideally a data-logging one, inside the pen at snail level in the shade. Record readings for at least two to four weeks during your driest month. Night-time averages below 75 % or daytime minimums below 50 % on many days mean you need moisture-retention systems. See the [Site Suitability Diagnostic Checklist](./08-site-suitability-diagnostic-checklist.md).

## Section 4: Soil

### What soil pH do snails need?
About 6.5–7.5 is ideal. Strongly acidic soil (below about 5.5) leads to brittle shells and slow growth. Strongly alkaline or salty soil (above about 8.5) is also unsuitable.

### How do I correct acidic soil for snails?
Mix in agricultural lime (calcium carbonate), crushed oyster shell or modest amounts of untreated wood ash. Let the soil settle for two to four weeks, then re-test. Never use quicklime or hydrated lime in occupied pens. See [Mitigation Systems](./09-mitigation-systems.md#5-soil-correction).

### Can I use soil from my crop farm for snail pens?
Only if it has not been treated with chemical fertilisers, pesticides or herbicides. Contaminated soil kills snails and can make the meat unsafe. Use untreated loamy topsoil, sieved to remove stones and ants.

### Is it safe to farm snails on oil-polluted or mining land?
No. Snails accumulate hydrocarbons and heavy metals from soil and feed, and these end up in the meat. Do not farm on, or take soil from, oil-polluted land, mining areas, roadsides, industrial sites or old dumps.

## Section 5: Mitigation and Climate Control

### Can I farm snails in a greenhouse in a hot country?
Yes, but only with external shade netting (commonly 50–70 %), strong ventilation and misting. An unshaded plastic tunnel in tropical sun can exceed 40–45 °C inside and kill snails. See [Mitigation Systems](./09-mitigation-systems.md#3-greenhouse-and-polytunnel-systems).

### How can I keep snails active during the dry season?
Shade the pens, put up windbreaks against dry winds, mulch the soil, water in the evening, add misting in covered pens and feed water-rich foods such as pawpaw, cucumber and watermelon rind. In marginal zones, move nurseries indoors.

### Is indoor snail farming profitable?
It can be, but only if the market price covers the extra cost of building, energy and water. Calculate realistic running costs against conservative dry-season prices. In very poor climates, trading, aggregating or processing snails from suitable regions is often more profitable than producing them locally.

### What is the cheapest way to raise humidity in snail pens?
Shade and mulch come first, then evening watering, then wet foam or coconut-coir pads in hutch boxes. Clay-pot (olla) irrigation and harvested rainwater keep costs low in dry areas.

### How do I keep snails warm in the highlands?
Use insulated housing with thermal mass, such as block walls, earth floors and black-painted water drums. Close tunnels or houses at dusk, and add thermostatically controlled heating where nights fall below about 15 °C. Avoid un-vented combustion heaters.

## Section 6: Markets and Business

### When are snail prices highest in West Africa?
In the dry season, roughly November to March, when wild snails are aestivating and hard to find. The Christmas and New Year festive period adds a further demand peak. Prices are lowest in the rainy season, when wild-collected snails flood the market.

### How are snails sold in Nigeria and Ghana?
Mostly live, by the piece, in size grades such as "small", "medium", "big" and "jumbo". Grades vary by market. Sales by weight are increasing among processors and formal buyers.

### Is there a market for snails in Kenya and Uganda?
Yes, but it is still being built. Main buyers include West African diaspora communities, hotels and restaurants, expatriates, exporters and snail-mucin (cosmetics) processors. Secure written buyer commitments before scaling up.

### What is snail mucin and is it a good business?
Snail mucin (slime) is collected from live snails and used in skincare products. It is a growing outlet across Africa, especially where local demand for snail meat is low. Success depends on hygienic, standards-compliant processing and reliable buyers.

### Can I export live giant African land snails?
Generally no. Many importing countries, including the United States, prohibit live giant African land snails as invasive pests. Processed snail meat or mucin may be exportable if it meets the importing country's food-safety and sanitary rules.

### Are snail-farming investment schemes with buy-back guarantees safe?
Treat them with great caution. Several schemes in Africa have collapsed after selling overpriced stock with promises of guaranteed buy-back. Verify buyers independently, visit farms that are actually selling, and take legal advice before signing.

## Section 7: Regulation

### Do I need a licence to farm snails in Kenya?
Yes. Land snails are treated as wildlife in Kenya, and commercial snail farming has required authorisation from the Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS). Contact KWS before buying stock or building pens to confirm current requirements.

### Do I need a permit to farm snails in South Africa?
Possibly. It depends on the species and province. Alien species are regulated under NEM:BA's Alien and Invasive Species Regulations, and provincial conservation authorities regulate indigenous fauna. Confirm requirements with DFFE and your provincial authority before acquiring stock.

### Can I import breeding snails from another country?
Only with all the necessary permits, such as wildlife, alien-species, veterinary or phytosanitary, and customs permits. This is especially important for non-native species. Never import snails informally: escaped snails can become serious agricultural pests.

### What should I do with dead snails and farm waste?
Never dump snails, eggs or pen soil containing eggs into the wild. Bury or compost waste in a controlled way. Crushed shells can be reused as a calcium supplement once cleaned and heat-treated.
