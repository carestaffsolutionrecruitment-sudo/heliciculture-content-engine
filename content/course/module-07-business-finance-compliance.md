# Module 7: Business Operations, Farm Financials and Regulatory Compliance

**Module objective:** Set up the snail farm as a legal, well-documented business, model its finances realistically and plan its growth.

**Commercial outcome:** A registered enterprise with a financial model, cash-flow forecast and record-keeping system ready for lenders, investors, buyers and inspectors.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify the registrations, licences and permissions a snail enterprise needs.
- Build a capital and operating cost model and calculate break-even and return per square metre.
- Meet food safety, traceability and record-keeping standards.
- Plan the transition from smallholder to commercial supplier.

## Lesson 7.1: Setting Up a Commercial Snail Enterprise: Registration, Licensing and Land Use

Requirements differ by country and change over time. Use this list as a map of what to check, not as legal advice.

| Requirement | What to check | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Business registration** | Legal form (sole trader, partnership, company, cooperative) and tax registration | UK: HMRC / Companies House; Nigeria: Corporate Affairs Commission; Ghana: Office of the Registrar of Companies |
| **Species and wildlife law** | Whether the species may be kept and farmed; rules on release and escape; protected species | Great Britain: Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 (s.14 release of non-native species; Schedule 5 protects *Helix pomatia*) |
| **Food business registration or approval** | Registration before trading; approval for some processing activities | UK: register with the local authority at least 28 days before trading; Nigeria: NAFDAC product registration for packaged foods; Ghana: Food and Drugs Authority |
| **Food hygiene law for snails** | Slaughter in an establishment; hepatopancreas removal | GB and EU: Regulation (EC) No 853/2004, Annex III, Section XI |
| **Animal by-products** | Disposal of mortalities and shells; by-product uses | GB and EU: Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009 |
| **Land use and planning** | Planning consent for buildings and polytunnels; agricultural land designation | Local planning authority |
| **Environmental permits** | Wash-water and effluent discharge | Environmental regulator; water company trade-effluent agreement |
| **Cosmetics** (if selling mucin) | Ingredient and product rules; Responsible Person | UK Cosmetics Regulation; EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 |
| **Employment and health and safety** | Contracts, risk assessments, insurance | National employment and health and safety law |

**Insurance:** public and product liability, stock (mortality) cover where available, buildings and equipment, and employer's liability if you have staff.

## Lesson 7.2: Capital and Operating Cost Modelling: Setup Costs vs Projected Return per Square Metre

**Capital expenditure (capex)**

- Land preparation, fencing and anti-escape systems
- Buildings, polytunnels or pens; racking and boxes
- Climate control: misting, heating or cooling, ventilation, loggers and alarms
- Processing equipment: boilers, stainless tables, chiller, freezer, vacuum packer
- Initial breeding stock and quarantine setup
- Working capital to cover the first production cycle

**Operating expenditure (opex), per cycle or per year**

- Feed (meal and forage)
- Substrate, disinfectants and consumables
- Energy (heating, cooling, misting, cold storage)
- Labour (daily care, harvesting, processing, record-keeping)
- Testing, compliance, insurance and veterinary advice
- Packaging, transport and marketing
- Repairs, maintenance and depreciation

**Key formulas**

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Saleable output per cycle (kg) | snails stocked × survival rate × average harvest weight (kg) |
| Revenue per cycle | Σ (saleable output for each product × price), including by-products |
| Gross margin per cycle | revenue − variable costs |
| Gross margin per m² | gross margin ÷ productive area (m²) |
| Break-even price per kg | (fixed costs per cycle + variable costs per cycle) ÷ saleable kg |
| Simple payback (years) | total capex ÷ annual net cash flow |
| Return on investment (%) | annual net profit ÷ total capex × 100 |

**Model conservatively.** Use local quotes for every cost and confirmed buyer prices for revenue. Assume higher mortality and a longer cycle in year one. Include a learning curve of one to two cycles before performance stabilises. Run a sensitivity analysis: how do the margin and payback change if survival falls by 10 points, the cycle lengthens by a month, or the price falls by 15 %?

## Lesson 7.3: Food Safety Regulations, Traceability and Record-Keeping Standards

**Food safety management.** Run a documented, HACCP-based system. The critical steps include:

- intake inspection;
- purging;
- conditioning temperature;
- dispatch;
- hepatopancreas removal;
- cooling, chilling and freezing temperatures;
- allergen labelling.

**Traceability ("one step back, one step forward").** Every batch of product must be traceable to its source unit, hatch or purchase batch, feed lots and processing date, and forward to every customer it was sold to.

**Records to keep** (at least 2 years, or longer if the law requires):

| Area | Records |
|---|---|
| Stock | Purchases, sources, quarantine, hatch dates, batch IDs |
| Husbandry | Climate logs, feed lots, densities, growth samples |
| Health | Mortalities, treatments, veterinary visits, disposal |
| Processing | Batch records, temperatures, cleaning, staff training |
| Sales | Invoices, customer, batch IDs, delivery temperatures |

## Lesson 7.4: Scaling Up: From Smallholder to Commercial Supplier

**Stage gates.** Scale only when each gate is met:

1. **Proof of production:** two consecutive cycles at or above target survival and growth.
2. **Proof of market:** repeat orders, or a written offtake agreement covering the added volume.
3. **Proof of systems:** complete records, SOPs followed and food safety registration in place.
4. **Proof of finance:** the expansion is funded without risking working capital for current production.

**Routes to scale**

- **Vertical:** add a controlled breeding room for year-round hatching.
- **Horizontal:** replicate proven pen modules.
- **Collective:** form or join a producer cooperative or outgrower scheme to aggregate volume for larger buyers. This is especially effective for smallholders, and for widening access for women and youth.
- **Downstream:** add processing and by-products (Modules 5 and 6) to capture more value per snail.

**Social impact:** snail farming has a small land footprint and low feed costs and produces a protein-rich meat, so it can support household nutrition and rural incomes. Track jobs created, the share of production sold into local markets, and producer income alongside profit if you seek development finance or impact investment.

## Practical Deliverable: Commercial Snail Farm Financial Model and Cash Flow Template

A spreadsheet with:

1. **Assumptions:** area, species, stocking density, survival rate, cycle length, harvest weight, prices by product and channel, feed rate and cost, labour hours and rate, energy use.
2. **Capex schedule:** items, cost, useful life and depreciation.
3. **Opex per cycle:** variable and fixed costs.
4. **Production and revenue:** saleable kg and by-product volumes per cycle.
5. **Monthly cash flow (36 months):** opening balance, receipts, payments and closing balance, flagging the months where the balance goes negative.
6. **Key metrics:** gross margin per m², break-even price, payback and ROI.
7. **Sensitivity table:** survival, cycle length and price scenarios.

## SOP / Technical Focus: Records and Traceability

1. **Assign batch IDs** at hatch or purchase, and carry them through every record.
2. **Complete daily logs** (climate, feed, mortality) before the end of each shift.
3. **Link each harvest** to its source batch, and each processed lot to its harvest batch.
4. **Record batch IDs** on every invoice and delivery note.
5. **Back up digital records weekly.** Keep paper records in a dry, secure place.
6. **Run a traceability test every quarter:** pick a sold batch and trace it back to its source and forward to every customer within 4 hours. Record the result.
7. **Review compliance every year:** registrations, permits, insurance and changes in regulation.
