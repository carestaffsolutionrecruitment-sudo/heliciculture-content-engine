# Module 3: Nutrition, Feeding Formulation and Growth Management

**Module objective:** Feed snails a balanced, calcium-rich diet efficiently and hygienically, and stock them at densities that maximise growth.

**Commercial outcome:** A costed feed formula, a feeding routine and a stocking plan that together cut the cycle to market weight and reduce feed waste.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why calcium is the limiting nutrient in snail production and how to supply it.
- Formulate a balanced dry meal from local ingredients and combine it with fresh forage.
- Run a hygienic feeding and waste-removal routine.
- Calculate and adjust stocking density for each production stage.

---

## Lesson 3.1: Calcium Dynamics: Mineral Sources, Grit and Shell Strengthening

Calcium is used for shell growth, egg shells and many metabolic functions. Snails cannot grow their shells faster than calcium is supplied, so calcium shortage shows up as slow growth, thin or brittle shells, chipped lips and poor egg production.

**Calcium sources**

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| Feed-grade limestone (calcium carbonate) | About 38–40 % calcium; cheap; the standard base |
| Ground oyster or cockle shell | About 35–38 % calcium; widely available in coastal areas |
| Dicalcium phosphate | Supplies calcium and phosphorus; use in small amounts |
| Sterilised bone meal | Calcium and phosphorus; check animal by-product rules before use |
| Cuttlefish bone (free choice) | Useful in breeding boxes |
| Wood ash | **Avoid.** Too alkaline and variable |

**Strengthening shells.** Supply calcium continuously in the feed (10–13 % of dry matter for *Cornu aspersum*), and also as a free-choice supplement, such as a dish of crushed limestone or a cuttlebone, in nursery and breeding units. Shell quality also depends on substrate pH (Module 2) and on avoiding acidic feeds.

## Lesson 3.2: Feed Formulation: Balancing Protein, Energy, Calcium and Water

A good snail diet combines a **formulated dry meal** with **fresh forage**. The meal supplies concentrated protein, energy and calcium; the forage supplies water, fibre and palatability.

**Example grower formula: *Cornu aspersum* (per 100 kg)**

| Ingredient | kg |
|---|---|
| Ground maize | 28 |
| Soya bean meal (~46 % crude protein) | 25 |
| Wheat bran | 12 |
| Dried lucerne meal | 4 |
| Calcium carbonate (feed-grade limestone) | 28 |
| Dicalcium phosphate | 2 |
| Salt-free vitamin–mineral premix | 1 |
| **Approximate analysis** | **~16–17 % crude protein, ~11–12 % calcium** |

**Example grower formula: *Achatina* group (per 100 kg)**

| Ingredient | kg |
|---|---|
| Ground maize | 31 |
| Soya bean meal (~46 % crude protein) | 30 |
| Wheat offal / middlings | 13 |
| Calcium carbonate or ground oyster shell | 20 |
| Sterilised bone meal (compliant source) | 5 |
| Salt-free vitamin–mineral premix | 1 |
| **Approximate analysis** | **~19 % crude protein, ~9 % calcium** |

**Fresh forage.** For *Cornu*, feed lucerne, clover, chard, cabbage and carrot tops. For *Achatina*, feed pawpaw, banana and cocoyam leaves, ripe pawpaw and other fruit where available. Wash forage and make sure it is free from pesticide residues. Avoid raw cassava leaves, which contain cyanogenic compounds, and anything mouldy.

**Formulation rules**

- **Never add salt:** it is toxic to snails.
- **Use feed-grade ingredients** from suppliers registered under feed hygiene rules, and keep the batch numbers.
- **Test, don't assume:** have the meal analysed for protein, calcium and phosphorus, and check for mycotoxins (aflatoxin) wherever maize or groundnut is used.
- **Adjust to growth:** if growth stalls despite good climate and density, check calcium first, then protein.

## Lesson 3.3: Feeding Frequency, Hygiene and Waste Removal

- **When to feed:** in the late afternoon or at the start of the dark period, when snails become active.
- **How to feed:** put dry meal in shallow troughs or on trays, never scattered on soil, so intake can be measured and residues removed.
- **How much:** start with roughly 3–5 % of the batch's live weight per day as meal for growers. Adjust daily so that only a small residue remains next morning.
- **Water:** provide it by misting and through saturated sponges or very shallow dishes. Hatchlings drown in open water.
- **Waste removal:** every morning, remove uneaten meal, wilted forage, droppings and dead snails. Fermenting residue is the main driver of bacterial outbreaks.
- **Weekly:** wash and dry the troughs. Store feed off the ground in sealed, rodent-proof bins, and use it oldest first.

## Lesson 3.4: Stocking Density Optimisation Across Hatchling, Grower and Breeder Stages

Overcrowding slows growth. Snails compete for feed, and a crowding effect carried in mucus and faeces suppresses growth even when feed is plentiful. Count usable surface (floor, walls, shelters, tray sides) rather than floor area alone.

| Stage | *Cornu aspersum* | *Achatina* group |
|---|---|---|
| Hatchlings / nursery | Up to ~1,500–2,000 per m² | Up to ~100 per m² |
| Growers / fattening | ~300–500 per m² (outdoor pens) | ~20–50 per m² |
| Breeders | ~100–150 per m² | ~5–10 per m² |

**Grading.** Sort snails by size every 3–4 weeks. Larger snails outcompete smaller ones at the trough, so grading improves uniformity and harvest planning.

**When growth stalls,** reduce density first, then check feed and climate.

## Practical Deliverable: Weekly Feed Formulation and Stocking Density Calculator

A spreadsheet with three tabs:

**1. Feed formulation tab**

- **Inputs:** each ingredient's kg per 100 kg, its crude protein %, calcium % and phosphorus %, and its price per kg.
- **Calculations:**
  - Formula crude protein % = Σ (kg × ingredient protein %) ÷ total kg
  - Formula calcium % = Σ (kg × ingredient calcium %) ÷ total kg
  - Cost per kg of feed = Σ (kg × price per kg) ÷ total kg
- **Checks:** flags the formula if protein or calcium falls outside the species targets, or if salt is included.

**2. Weekly feed requirement tab**

- **Inputs:** number of snails per unit, average live weight (g) and feeding rate (% of live weight per day).
- **Calculations:**
  - Daily meal (kg) = number × average weight (g) × feeding rate ÷ 100 ÷ 1,000
  - Weekly meal (kg) = daily meal × 7
  - Weekly feed cost = weekly meal × cost per kg

**3. Stocking density tab**

- **Inputs:** usable surface area (m²) for each unit, and the stage.
- **Calculations:**
  - Current density = number ÷ area
  - Maximum stock = area × target density for the stage
  - Surplus to move = current number − maximum stock (if positive)
- **Checks:** highlights any unit above its stage's target density.

**Growth tracking:** log the fortnightly sample weights to produce a growth curve and a feed conversion ratio (meal fed ÷ live weight gained) for each batch.

## SOP / Technical Focus: Daily Feeding and Hygiene Round

1. **Morning:** remove residues, droppings and dead snails from every trough and unit. Record mortalities by unit.
2. Estimate the residue left from the previous feed (none, small or large), and adjust today's ration by −10 %, 0 or +10 %.
3. **Afternoon:** weigh out and place the dry meal in clean troughs, then add washed fresh forage.
4. Mist or water as the climate targets require (Module 2).
5. **Weekly:** wash the troughs, check the feed store for damp, mould and rodents, and rotate stock.
6. **Fortnightly:** sample-weigh 30–50 snails per batch and update the growth curve.
7. **Every 3–4 weeks:** grade by size and rebalance densities.

## Operational Checklist

- [ ] Feed formula analysed for protein and calcium
- [ ] No added salt in feed, supplements or cleaning products used on live stock
- [ ] Feed stored off the ground in sealed, rodent-proof containers
- [ ] Residues removed daily
- [ ] Densities within targets for each stage
- [ ] Growth curve updated fortnightly

## Knowledge Check

1. What is the first nutrient to check when shells are thin and growth is slow? *(Calcium.)*
2. Why should feed meal not be scattered on the soil? *(Intake can't be measured and residues ferment, driving bacterial disease.)*
3. What is the first action when growth stalls in a well-fed unit? *(Reduce stocking density.)*
