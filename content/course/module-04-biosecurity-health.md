# Module 4: Farm Biosecurity, Veterinary Health and Disease Management

**Module objective:** Prevent disease entering the farm, recognise problems early and contain outbreaks before they spread.

**Commercial outcome:** A documented biosecurity programme with quarantine, sanitation and mortality records that protects yield, satisfies food safety audits and supports insurance claims.

## Learning Objectives

- Recognise the main pathogens, parasites and clinical signs in farmed snails.
- Run a quarantine protocol for all incoming stock.
- Maintain sanitation and temperature control for stores and processing equipment.
- Track mortality, set culling thresholds and contain outbreaks.

---

## Lesson 4.1: Pathogen and Parasite Identification

Most disease in snail farming is a consequence of husbandry. Wet, dirty, crowded and stagnant conditions are the main causes. Learn the agents, but fix the conditions.

**Bacteria**

- ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and other opportunistic bacteria.** These are associated with intestinal infection and clustered deaths, especially in wet, fouled units. Signs include lethargy, loss of appetite, a discoloured or foul-smelling body and sudden mortality in one box or pen.

**Mites (arthropods, not nematodes)**

- ***Riccardoella* mites**, including *R. oudemansi* in *Cornu aspersum*, are tiny white mites in the lung (mantle) cavity and on the body. They reduce growth and egg production, spread readily in crowded units and survive in untreated timber. They can be seen as moving white specks on the body near the pneumostome, best under a hand lens.

**Nematodes (worms)**

- ***Phasmarhabditis hermaphrodita*** is sold as a commercial slug biocontrol and can infect and kill some snails, juveniles especially. Its main risk comes from neighbouring land, so it must never be used on or near a snail farm.
- ***Angiostrongylus cantonensis*** (rat lungworm) uses snails as an intermediate host. It is a human-health risk rather than a snail disease, and is a key reason to source captive-bred stock and always cook snails thoroughly.

**Clinical signs to recognise**

- **Mantle recession ("mantle collapse"):** the mantle edge pulls back from the shell lip. This is a term used mainly by keepers, not a specific disease. It signals serious stress or illness, commonly from dehydration, calcium shortage, bacterial infection or chemical exposure. Treat it as an urgent warning and investigate the conditions.
- **Shell damage and thinning:** usually calcium deficiency, acidic substrate or rough handling.
- **Excessive or discoloured mucus, foul smell, retraction failure:** possible infection or chemical exposure.
- **Widespread epiphragm sealing:** environmental stress (Module 2).

## Lesson 4.2: Quarantine and Biosecurity Protocols for New Stock

- **Zoning:** divide the site into clean (breeding, hatching, nursery), production (fattening) and dirty (dispatch, waste, deliveries) zones. People and equipment move from clean to dirty, never the reverse, without cleaning.
- **Quarantine:** hold all incoming stock in a separate room for **at least 4–6 weeks**, with dedicated tools, footwear and clothing.
- **Inspection:** check daily for mortality, mites, abnormal mucus and behaviour. Release the stock only if it shows no signs of disease.
- **Sourcing:** buy from one documented, captive-bred source where possible. Never mix wild-collected snails with farmed stock.
- **Visitors:** keep a visitor log, require dedicated footwear or overshoes, and allow no unnecessary access to breeding rooms.

## Lesson 4.3: Sanitation, Cold Store Temperature Auditing and Preventive Hygiene

**Cleaning and disinfection.** Between batches, empty, scrape and wash boxes, trays and troughs. Disinfect them with a product approved for food-animal environments, then rinse thoroughly. Snails are highly sensitive to chemical residues, so let everything dry fully before restocking.

**Cold store and freezer auditing.** Farms that hold feed, purging stock or processed product must control temperatures:

| Equipment | Target | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Conditioning chiller (live *Cornu* before dispatch) | 2–6 °C | Twice daily |
| Chilled storage of processed meat | 0–5 °C | Twice daily |
| Freezer | −18 °C or colder | Daily |
| Feed store | Cool, dry, below ~25 °C | Weekly |

Record every reading. If a reading is out of range, record the corrective action and decide whether the affected product is still safe. Calibrate thermometers every quarter.

**Preventive hygiene:** handwashing stations at zone entrances; gloves when handling raw *Achatina*; no eating or drinking in production areas; pest control records; clean, dry footwear.

## Lesson 4.4: Mortality Tracking, Culling Thresholds and Outbreak Containment

**Track every death.** Remove dead snails daily, count them by unit and dispose of them through an approved animal by-product route. A sustained rise in mortality is your earliest disease signal.

**Indicative action thresholds** (calibrate them to your own baseline):

| Daily mortality in a unit | Status | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Below baseline (often under ~0.2 % per day in established growers) | Normal | Continue routine |
| 2× baseline for 2 consecutive days | Alert | Review climate logs, feed, residues and density; inspect closely |
| 5× baseline, or clustered deaths | Outbreak | Isolate the unit and start the containment steps below |

**Culling.** Remove and humanely dispatch snails that are moribund, unable to retract or badly shell-damaged, using the method in Module 5. Never return them to the population.

**Outbreak containment steps**

1. Stop all movement of snails, equipment and staff from the affected unit.
2. Remove and record all dead and moribund snails.
3. Check and correct humidity, ventilation, residues and density.
4. Send samples to a veterinary diagnostic laboratory if available, and notify your veterinary adviser.
5. Clean and disinfect the unit, and replace or heat-treat the substrate.
6. Keep the unit isolated until mortality returns to baseline for at least 2 weeks.
7. Write up the cause and lessons learned.

## Practical Deliverable: Mortality and Sanitation Audit Log

A printable or digital log with:

- **Daily mortality** by unit, with a running 7-day average and automatic alert and outbreak flags.
- **Cleaning and disinfection** records: unit, date, product, contact time, rinse confirmed and staff initials.
- **Cold store and freezer temperatures** with corrective actions.
- **Quarantine register:** batch, source, arrival date, inspections and release decision.
- **Visitor log.**

## SOP / Technical Focus: Biosecurity Sanitation and Mortality Audit

**Daily**

1. Enter zones in clean-to-dirty order. Wash your hands and change footwear at each zone boundary.
2. Remove dead snails, record the count per unit and bag them for approved disposal.
3. Record chiller and freezer temperatures, plus corrective actions.
4. Check the mortality log for alert or outbreak flags, and escalate the same day if any are triggered.

**Weekly**

5. Review the 7-day mortality trend for every unit with the farm manager.
6. Inspect quarantine stock and update the register.
7. Check pest control stations and record findings.

**Between batches**

8. Empty, clean, disinfect, rinse and dry each unit, then record the details.
9. Replace or heat-treat the substrate.

**Monthly**

10. Audit the logs for completeness, and sign off the review.
