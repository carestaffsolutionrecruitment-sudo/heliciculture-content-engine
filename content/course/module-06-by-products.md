# Module 6: Advanced By-Product Value Addition (Mucin, Eggs and Shells)

**Module objective:** Turn mucin, eggs and shells into additional revenue streams, produced to the standards their markets and regulators require.

**Commercial outcome:** A by-product plan that adds income per square metre without compromising breeding stock, animal welfare or legal compliance.

## Learning Objectives

- Collect snail mucin ethically and process it to cosmetic-ingredient standards.
- Produce snail caviar safely, from egg collection to packaging.
- Process shells for animal feed and craft uses within animal by-product rules.
- Model the revenue and costs of each by-product stream.

---

## Lesson 6.1: Snail Mucin Harvesting: Ethical Collection, Filtration, Stabilisation and Cosmetic Standards

Snail mucin is a widely used skincare ingredient, listed on cosmetic labels as *Snail Secretion Filtrate*. It is a **cosmetic ingredient, not a food**, and is regulated under cosmetics law: the UK Cosmetics Regulation in Great Britain, Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 in the EU, and national equivalents elsewhere.

**Ethical collection principles**

- Use healthy, purged, well-hydrated adult snails.
- Use non-lethal methods that stimulate secretion gently. Never use salt, acids or other irritants.
- Limit collection frequency, return snails to normal husbandry between collections, and monitor weight and mortality in collection groups.
- Never combine mucin collection with kitchen purging.

**Processing chain**

1. **Collect** into sterile, food-grade containers in a clean, dedicated room.
2. **Filter** coarsely to remove debris, then finely (staged filtration down to microfiltration).
3. **Stabilise** with a cosmetic-grade preservative system, chosen with the formulator or laboratory.
4. **Test** each batch for microbial load, pH, appearance and stability.
5. **Store and label** in sealed, light-protected containers with the batch number, date and storage conditions.

**Compliance:** finished cosmetic products need a safety assessment and a designated Responsible Person. If you sell raw filtrate to cosmetic manufacturers, they will require batch documentation, test certificates and consistent supply. "Cruelty-free" and "ethically sourced" claims must be substantiated.

## Lesson 6.2: Snail Caviar Production: Harvesting, Brining, Curing and Packaging

Snail caviar is the eggs of *Cornu aspersum* or related species. The pearly, white to off-white spheres are about 3–4 mm across, with a delicate earthy, mushroom-like flavour. It is a premium product, sometimes marketed as "white gold".

1. **Egg laying:** breeders lay into prepared pots of moist, sterilised substrate.
2. **Collection:** collect clutches promptly, ideally within 24 hours of laying, before embryonic development.
3. **Cleaning and sorting:** separate the eggs from the substrate, wash them gently, and discard broken or discoloured eggs.
4. **Brining and curing:** cure in a measured brine, sometimes with herbs or aromatics, to a recipe developed with a food technologist.
5. **Pasteurising and packing:** pack into sterilised glass jars and pasteurise using a validated time and temperature, then chill. The product is not shelf-stable at room temperature.
6. **Labelling:** batch, use-by date, storage conditions and the mollusc allergen.

**Compliance:** confirm the product's food-law status with your national food authority, including any novel food considerations, before launch. Validate the process and shelf life with a qualified food technologist.

**Commercial trade-off:** every clutch sold as caviar is a clutch that won't become replacement stock. Plan caviar volumes alongside your breeding targets.

## Lesson 6.3: Shell Processing: Calcium for Animal Feed and Craft Applications

Shells are about 95 % calcium carbonate, which makes them a valuable mineral source.

**Animal feed (for example poultry layer grit)**

1. Collect shells after processing, separately from soft tissue.
2. Wash off residual tissue and mucus.
3. Heat-treat them, for example by drying or baking at high temperature, to destroy pathogens.
4. Crush and grade to the particle size the buyer needs (coarse grit for layers).
5. Store dry, in sealed bags, labelled with the batch.

**Regulatory note:** in Great Britain and the EU, shells and other processing waste are **animal by-products** under Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009 and its GB equivalent. Their use in feed for farmed animals is controlled. Confirm the required category, processing standard and approvals with your national authority before selling shell grit as feed. Never feed processed snail material back to snails. Other countries have their own feed and by-product rules.

**Craft and other uses:** cleaned, sterilised whole shells sell to restaurants (for service), craft and jewellery makers, and garden lime products.

## Lesson 6.4: Economic Yield Analysis: Revenue Beyond Meat

For each by-product stream, calculate:

- **Gross revenue** = units sold × price
- **Direct costs** = labour + materials + testing + packaging + energy + compliance
- **Contribution** = gross revenue − direct costs
- **Contribution per m²** = contribution ÷ production area allocated
- **Opportunity cost:** for caviar, the value of the hatchlings not produced; for mucin, any reduction in the collection group's growth or breeding performance.

**Decision rule:** add a by-product only if its contribution is positive after compliance costs, and only if it doesn't compromise breeding stock or animal welfare. Start with the stream that needs the least new compliance work. For many farms that is shell processing, then caviar, then mucin.

## SOP / Technical Focus: By-Product Hygiene and Traceability

1. **Assign a batch ID** linking each by-product batch to its source unit and date.
2. **Process in a dedicated, cleaned area,** separate from live-animal and raw-meat zones.
3. **Follow the validated process** exactly, and record the critical parameters: filtration grade, preservative dose, brine strength, pasteurisation time and temperature, heat-treatment temperature.
4. **Test** each batch as specified, and hold it until the results are acceptable.
5. **Label and store** under the stated conditions, and log storage temperatures where relevant.
6. **Record** the dispatch: customer, quantity and batch IDs. Keep records for at least 2 years, or longer if the law requires.
