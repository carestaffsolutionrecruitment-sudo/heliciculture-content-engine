# Module 8: Marketing, Sales Channels and Distribution Networks

**Module objective:** Find and win the right buyers, build a trusted brand, and deliver product safely to market.

**Commercial outcome:** A channel strategy, a wholesale price list, a B2B pitch and a cold chain that together turn production into reliable revenue.

## Learning Objectives

- Segment target markets and match products to each.
- Build digital brand authority through expert, trustworthy content.
- Package and transport live and processed snails safely.
- Understand export requirements and the essentials of a B2B wholesale contract.

---

## Lesson 8.1: Identifying Target Markets

| Segment | What they buy | What they value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High-end restaurants** | Cleaned, cooked or frozen meat; shells for service; caviar | Consistency, size grading, traceability, reliable delivery | Small volumes, premium prices; build relationships with chefs |
| **Ethnic and specialist grocers** | Live *Achatina* (where legal), frozen cleaned meat | Price, size, freshness, familiar species | Strong demand in West African diaspora markets |
| **Processors and wholesalers** | Live or frozen bulk product | Volume, price, specifications, documentation | Lower margins; requires steady supply |
| **Cosmetic laboratories** | Snail secretion filtrate | Batch documentation, test certificates, consistent quality | Specialist, regulated; contract-based |
| **Direct to consumer** | Prepared snails, recipe kits, caviar, farm visits | Story, convenience, provenance | Higher margin; more marketing effort |
| **Institutional and community buyers** | Affordable frozen meat | Price, nutrition, safety | Supports local food security; may be linked to nutrition programmes |

**Choose one or two primary channels to start.** Match each channel to a product form, a price point and a delivery capability you can sustain.

## Lesson 8.2: Building Digital Brand Authority: Content Marketing and E-E-A-T

Search engines and AI answer engines increasingly reward content that shows **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T)**. For a snail farm, that means:

- **Experience:** photographs and video of your own farm, real production data, honest accounts of problems you solved.
- **Expertise:** accurate, well-sourced technical content (as in this course and the Snail World glossary) attributed to named, qualified authors.
- **Authoritativeness:** listings on recognised directories (such as Snail World), industry association membership, citations from trusted sites.
- **Trustworthiness:** clear contact details, registration numbers, food safety credentials, transparent pricing and policies.

**Practical content plan**

1. A farm profile page with a named owner, location, species, certifications and contact details.
2. Product pages with specifications, storage instructions, allergen information and order details.
3. Regular articles answering buyers' real questions: how to cook, how to store, how snails are raised.
4. Structured data (`Organization`, `Product`, `FAQPage`, `Article`) so search engines understand your pages.
5. Reviews and case studies from restaurant and retail customers, used with their permission.

## Lesson 8.3: Cold Chain Logistics and Packaging for Transport

**Live snails**

- ***Cornu aspersum*:** transport dormant snails in ventilated mesh bags or crates, kept cool (roughly 4–10 °C), protected from crushing and direct sun, for the shortest possible time.
- ***Achatina* group:** never chill. Use ventilated, humid containers at moderate temperatures (roughly 18–26 °C), with no direct sun or heat build-up in vehicles.
- **Escape-proof packaging:** sealed, labelled with the species, origin, quantity and date. Check live-animal transport rules for the route.

**Processed product**

| Product | Temperature | Packaging |
|---|---|---|
| Chilled meat | 0–5 °C | Sealed food-grade trays or vacuum packs in insulated boxes with ice packs |
| Frozen meat | −18 °C or colder | Vacuum packs in insulated boxes with frozen gel packs or dry ice; frozen vehicles for distance |
| Caviar | Chilled as per label | Glass jars in protective, insulated packaging |

**Cold chain controls:** pre-chill vehicles and boxes, use temperature loggers or indicators for longer routes, record the temperature at dispatch and on delivery, and agree with the buyer what happens if a delivery arrives out of temperature.

## Lesson 8.4: Export Compliance and B2B Wholesale Contracts

**Export essentials** (requirements are set by the importing country; confirm them before quoting):

- **Is the product admissible?** Many countries ban live giant African land snails outright. Processed snail meat may be admissible only from approved establishments in authorised countries. For example, the EU accepts snails for human consumption only from listed countries and approved establishments, with official health certificates.
- **Documentation:** health or veterinary certificates, certificates of origin, commercial invoice, packing list and any import permits.
- **Labelling:** in the importing country's language, with allergen declarations and storage instructions.
- **Residue and contaminant testing** where the importer requires it.

**B2B wholesale contract essentials**

- Product specification: species, form, size grade, packaging and labelling
- Volume, frequency and minimum order quantities
- Price, currency, price-review terms and payment terms
- Delivery terms (for example Incoterms), cold chain responsibilities and temperature tolerances
- Quality acceptance, rejection procedure and claims window
- Documentation and traceability obligations
- Force majeure, termination and dispute resolution

## Practical Deliverable: B2B Pitch Deck Outline and Wholesale Pricing Guide

**Pitch deck outline (10 slides)**

1. Who we are: farm, founders, location, mission
2. The product: species, forms, size grades, photos
3. Quality and safety: registrations, HACCP, traceability, testing
4. Production capacity and consistency: volumes per month, year-round supply
5. Sustainability and impact: low footprint, local jobs, nutrition
6. Specifications sheet: weights, packaging, shelf life, storage
7. Logistics: cold chain, delivery area, lead times
8. Pricing and terms summary
9. Customer testimonials or pilot results
10. Next steps: samples, trial order and contact details

**Wholesale pricing guide**

| Step | Formula |
|---|---|
| Full cost per kg | (production + processing + packaging + delivery + overhead per cycle) ÷ saleable kg |
| Minimum wholesale price | full cost per kg × (1 + target margin) |
| Channel price check | buyer's resale price × (1 − buyer's required margin) must be ≥ your minimum wholesale price |
| Volume tiers | discount bands only above volumes that lower your unit cost |

Review prices every quarter against feed, energy and transport costs.

## SOP / Technical Focus: Cold Chain Dispatch

1. **Confirm the order:** product, batch IDs, quantity, delivery date and temperature requirement.
2. **Pre-chill or pre-freeze** the insulated boxes and the vehicle. Prepare enough ice or gel packs for the journey time.
3. **Pick and check the product:** labels, use-by dates and packaging integrity.
4. **Record the product temperature at packing.** Live *Cornu* should be cool and dormant; live *Achatina* at a moderate temperature, never chilled.
5. **Pack, seal and label** each box with its contents, batch, storage instructions and orientation.
6. **Place a temperature logger** or indicator in each consignment on longer routes.
7. **Deliver,** and record the temperature on handover. Get the buyer's signature.
8. **Investigate any out-of-temperature delivery** immediately, and record the outcome and corrective action.
