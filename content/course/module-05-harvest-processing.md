# Module 5: Harvesting, Conditioning and Humane Processing

**Module objective:** Harvest snails at the right time, purge and condition them correctly, and process them humanely and hygienically to a safe, consistent product.

**Commercial outcome:** A repeatable harvest-to-pack process that meets food hygiene law, maximises usable yield and protects your reputation with buyers.

## Learning Objectives

- Judge market readiness by weight, shell maturity and age.
- Run the purge fast and the conditioning chill as two distinct, correctly timed stages.
- Dispatch snails humanely and extract the meat hygienically.
- Remove the hepatopancreas, clean the meat and preserve it safely.

---

## Lesson 5.1: Assessing Market Readiness: Weight, Lip Formation and Age

| Indicator | *Cornu aspersum* | *Achatina* group |
|---|---|---|
| Market weight | Petit-gris ~7–12 g; gros-gris ~15–25 g | Typically 100–300 g+, depending on buyer |
| Shell maturity | Thickened, flared lip (peristome) fully formed and hard | Firm, thick shell lip; growth in length has slowed |
| Typical age (controlled conditions) | ~4–7 months from hatching | ~12–24 months from hatching |

**Sampling for harvest:** weigh a random sample of 30–50 snails. Harvest a batch when at least 80–90 % meet the buyer's weight and maturity specification. Grade the rest back into growing units.

**Why the lip matters:** snails harvested before the lip hardens have thin, fragile shells and a lower meat yield, and some buyers reject them outright.

---

## Lesson 5.2: The Purge Fast and the Conditioning Chill

The course outline combines these into a single "24–48 hour conditioning and purging fast at 2–6 °C". They are two separate stages with different temperatures. Purging needs active snails, while chilling makes snails dormant, so a snail chilled from the start cannot purge.

**Stage 1: Purge fast** (snails active)

- **Purpose:** empty the gut of feed, soil and grit.
- **Conditions:** clean, perforated, ventilated containers. *Cornu* at about 15–20 °C; *Achatina* at about 22–26 °C. High humidity (mist once or twice a day) and containers rinsed daily.
- **Duration:** about 48–72 hours for farmed *Cornu*; about 24–72 hours for farmed *Achatina*. Wild-gathered snails need longer, often 2–5 days on bran or flour first, then 3–5 days of fasting.
- **Rules:** never add salt, vinegar or detergents to live snails.

**Stage 2: Conditioning chill** (snails become dormant)

- ***Cornu aspersum*:** 24–48 hours at **2–6 °C**. Snails become dormant, and many form an epiphragm.
- ***Achatina* group:** **do not chill to 2–6 °C.** These tropical animals tolerate cold poorly; use a short chill of several hours at about 8–12 °C.

**Intake inspection** (before Stage 1): discard any snail that is cracked, foul-smelling or fails to retract, and record the rejection rate.

## Lesson 5.3: Humane Dispatch and Hygienic Shell Extraction

Gastropods currently fall outside UK animal welfare legislation. Even so, there is credible evidence that molluscs respond to harmful stimuli, and buyers increasingly expect humane practice. Use a written dispatch procedure.

**Recommended method**

1. Chill to dormancy (Stage 2 above).
2. Bring a large volume of water to a full, rolling boil, at least 10 times the weight of the snails.
3. Add snails in small batches so the water returns to the boil almost immediately.
4. Boil for 3–5 minutes for *Cornu* or *Helix*, and 5–8 minutes for *Achatina* depending on size.
5. Drain and chill rapidly in iced water.

**Practices not to use**

- **Salting live snails:** salt dehydrates them and causes prolonged distress.
- **Starting snails in cold water,** which prolongs the time to death.
- **Slow freezing alive.**
- **Cracking or extracting live *Achatina*.** This is traditional in parts of West Africa, but in professional processing, dispatch first.

**Extraction:** draw the body out with a snail fork, following the spiral. Work over clean stainless-steel surfaces, keep the meat on ice and separate shells for by-product processing (Module 6).

## Lesson 5.4: Hepatopancreas Removal, Cleaning and Preservation

**Hepatopancreas removal.** Cut away the dark, coiled visceral mass, including the digestive gland (the *tortillon* in French kitchens), at its junction with the foot. In Great Britain and the EU, Regulation (EC) No 853/2004, Annex III, Section XI, requires it to be removed where it may present a hazard, and it must not then be used for food. Removal also prevents enzymatic autolysis, which softens texture and causes off-flavours. For *Achatina*, slit the foot and remove the intestine and remaining dark tissue.

**Cleaning (mucin removal), after dispatch only**

- ***Cornu* / *Helix*:** massage the meat with coarse salt and a little vinegar, then rinse under cold running water. Repeat until the water runs clear.
- ***Achatina*:** rub with lime juice or food-grade alum plus flour or cornmeal, then rinse repeatedly.

**Preservation options**

| Method | Key controls | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Chilled (raw or cooked in liquor) | 0–5 °C; validated shelf life (often up to ~3 days cooked) | Local restaurant supply |
| Vacuum-packed and frozen | Blast-freeze to −18 °C or colder; label with date and batch | Wholesale, distance markets |
| Cooked in court-bouillon, then chilled or frozen | Cool to below 8 °C within 90 minutes | Value-added, ready-to-use product |
| Thermal pasteurisation | Validated time and temperature, set with a food technologist; still needs chilling | Snail caviar, prepared products |
| Commercial canning (sterilisation) | Validated thermal process; specialist licensed facility only | Shelf-stable retail |

**Food safety essentials:** always cook snails thoroughly before serving; wear gloves when handling raw *Achatina*; keep raw and cooked separate; and label molluscs as an allergen.

## Practical Deliverable: Harvest and Processing Batch Record

A form for each batch recording:

- **Batch details:** batch ID, unit of origin, harvest date, number harvested and sample weights.
- **Purge:** start and end times, temperature and intake rejections.
- **Chill:** start and end times, temperature readings.
- **Dispatch:** time and water temperature confirmation.
- **Yield:**
  - meat weight after extraction and after cleaning;
  - meat yield % = clean meat ÷ live weight × 100.
- **Product:** preservation method, pack date, use-by date, storage temperature and dispatch destination.

## SOP / Technical Focus: Harvest, Purging and Processing Safety Protocol

1. **Harvest:** sample-weigh, confirm at least 80–90 % meet the specification, collect the snails and record the count.
2. **Intake inspection:** reject damaged or dead snails and record them.
3. **Purge:** purge at the species temperature for the set duration, with daily rinsing and misting. No salt, vinegar or detergents.
4. **Condition:** chill *Cornu* for 24–48 hours at 2–6 °C. Give *Achatina* a short chill at 8–12 °C. Log the temperatures.
5. **Prepare:** sanitise surfaces and tools, put on clean protective clothing and gloves, and keep raw and cooked areas separate.
6. **Dispatch:** small batches into a rolling boil, for the timed duration, then an ice bath.
7. **Extract and eviscerate:** remove the meat, the hepatopancreas and (for *Achatina*) the intestine. Keep the meat on ice.
8. **Clean:** remove the mucin with salt and vinegar, or lime, alum and flour, then rinse until clear.
9. **Preserve:** chill, freeze, cook or pasteurise according to the validated method, and label with the batch, date and allergen.
10. **Record:** complete the batch record and file it for traceability.

## Operational Checklist

- [ ] Harvest specification agreed with the buyer (weight, maturity, form)
- [ ] Purge and chill run as separate stages with logged temperatures
- [ ] Rolling-boil dispatch and ice bath ready before starting
- [ ] Hepatopancreas removed from every snail
- [ ] Chill or freeze targets met and logged
- [ ] Labels include the allergen (molluscs), batch and use-by date
