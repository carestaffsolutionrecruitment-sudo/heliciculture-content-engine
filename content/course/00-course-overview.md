# Commercial Heliculture: From Smallholder to Professional Snail Farm

**A Snail World professional course** · snailworld.org

## Course Overview

Snail farming (heliculture) turns low-cost plant feed and calcium into a high-value, protein-rich food on a small land footprint. It suits smallholders, women- and youth-led enterprises and diversifying farms, as well as intensive commercial units supplying restaurants, retailers and processors. Done well, it supports household food security and local nutrition alongside a viable business. Done badly, it loses stock to dehydration, disease and escape, and in the case of tropical species it can create a serious invasive-species liability.

This course takes you from snail biology to a running, compliant, profitable enterprise. It pairs scientific explanation with practical standards: every module ends with a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) and a downloadable tool you can use on your own farm.

## Species Covered

| Species | Common names | Typical system |
|---|---|---|
| *Cornu aspersum* (formerly *Helix aspersa*) | Petit-gris, gros-gris (*C. a. maxima*), brown garden snail | Temperate outdoor, polytunnel and indoor systems (UK, Europe, Mediterranean, temperate highlands) |
| *Archachatina marginata* | Giant West African snail, "big black" | Tropical pens and indoor units (West and Central Africa) |
| *Achatina achatina* | Giant Ghana / tiger snail | Tropical pens (West Africa) |
| *Lissachatina fulica* (formerly *Achatina fulica*) | Giant African land snail | **Restricted.** Covered for identification and biosecurity only; see below |

> **Important: legal status of giant African land snails.** *Lissachatina fulica* is one of the world's most damaging invasive species and a host of the rat lungworm parasite. Keeping, importing or farming it is prohibited in several countries, including the United States and Australia, and releasing any non-native snail into the wild is an offence in Great Britain and many other jurisdictions. Check your national rules before acquiring any *Achatina*-group stock. This course recommends *Cornu aspersum* for temperate regions and native *Archachatina marginata* or *Achatina achatina* within their native West African range.

## Important Notices

- **Regulation varies by country and changes over time.** The course names the frameworks to check (with UK/EU and West African examples) but is not legal advice. Confirm requirements with your national food safety, veterinary and environmental authorities.
- **Figures are indicative.** Temperatures, densities and feed formulae are evidence-based starting points. Calibrate them using your own production records.
- **Food safety.** Snails must always be cooked thoroughly. Molluscs are a declarable allergen in the UK, EU and many other markets.
