# SOP M8: Cold Chain Dispatch

| SOP No. | Version | Effective date | Review date | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SW-SOP-M8 | 1.0 | [date] | [date + 12 months] | Farm Manager / Dispatch Lead |

## 1. Purpose
To pack and ship live and processed snail products so that they arrive at the customer's kitchen or laboratory safe, within temperature, on time and fully traceable.

## 2. Scope
All dispatches: own-vehicle delivery, express courier, and refrigerated haulage.

## 3. Responsibilities
- **Dispatch operator:** steps 5.1–5.16.
- **Dispatch lead:** courier booking, route and timing decisions, and handling of delivery issues (5.17–5.20).

## 4. Safety Warnings
> ⚠️ **Dry ice (solid CO₂):** −78 °C, so it causes cold burns. Handle it with insulated gloves and eye protection. Never seal it in an airtight container (it can explode). Use only in well-ventilated areas and vehicles. Shipments containing dry ice must be declared and labelled under the carrier's dangerous goods rules (e.g. UN 1845).
>
> ⚠️ **Manual handling:** loaded insulated boxes are heavy. Lift correctly and use trolleys.
>
> ⚠️ **Live animals:** many couriers do not accept live animals, or only under specific services. Confirm in writing before booking. **Never ship live snails** where their sale or movement is prohibited (e.g. giant African land snails in the US), or without the required movement permits.
>
> ⚠️ **Food safety:** never dispatch product that is out of temperature, past its use-by date or carries the wrong label.

## 5. Procedure

### A. Order confirmation (day before dispatch)
5.1 Confirm the order: product, grade, quantity, Batch IDs to supply, delivery address, delivery window, and the customer's receiving contact.

5.2 **Plan the transit time.** Dispatch early in the week. Avoid shipments that would sit in a depot over a weekend or public holiday. Choose next-day or same-day services for chilled and live product.

5.3 **Book the courier or vehicle.** For live snails, confirm the service accepts them. For dry ice, confirm dangerous goods acceptance.

### B. Prepare packaging
5.4 **Pre-condition:**
- Freeze the gel packs for at least 24 hours (frozen product) or chill them (chilled product).
- Pre-chill the insulated boxes and vehicle for chilled and frozen loads.
- For live *Achatina*, use ambient packs, never frozen ones.

5.5 **Select packaging:**

| Product | Target temperature | Packaging | Coolant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live *Cornu* (dormant) | ~4–10 °C | Ventilated mesh bags or crates inside an insulated box, with ventilation holes | Chilled gel packs, wrapped, never in direct contact |
| Live *Achatina* | ~18–26 °C; humid | Ventilated, escape-proof crates, damp (not wet) packing material | None, or a moderating pack; **never chill** |
| Chilled meat / caviar | 0–5 °C | Sealed packs or jars in an insulated box (EPS or equivalent) | Chilled or frozen gel packs above and below the product |
| Frozen meat | −18 °C or colder | Vacuum packs in a thick-walled insulated box | Frozen gel packs or dry ice; frozen vehicle for long routes |

### C. Pick and check
5.6 Pick the product by Batch ID, oldest compliant batch first.

5.7 **Check every unit:** labels (product, Batch ID, dates, storage, allergen), packaging integrity, use-by date and appearance.

5.8 **Probe or measure the product temperature** before packing, and record it. Do not dispatch anything out of range.

### D. Pack
5.9 Line the box. Place coolant below the product, then the product, then coolant on top. Fill any gaps so the contents can't move. Keep coolant from touching live snails directly.

5.10 Place a **temperature data logger** (for routes over 2 hours, all export loads and all new customers) or a **temperature indicator** inside the box. Start the logger and record its ID.

5.11 Seal the box. For live snails, check that the ventilation is unobstructed.

5.12 **Label the outside:** customer, address, contents, Batch IDs, "Keep refrigerated / frozen / live animals – keep cool and upright" as appropriate, orientation arrows, sender contact, and any dry ice declaration.

5.13 Put the **delivery note** (with Batch IDs and dispatch temperature) and any required certificates in a document pouch on the box.

### E. Hand-over and transit
5.14 Record the dispatch time, the courier or driver, and the tracking number.

5.15 Send the customer a dispatch notice: tracking number, expected arrival, and instructions to check and record the temperature on receipt.

5.16 Own vehicle: pre-cooled, with the temperature monitored during the journey. Deliver directly, without intermediate stops that leave the cargo exposed.

### F. Delivery confirmation and issues
5.17 On delivery, get the **receipt temperature** and signature from the customer (or courier proof of delivery plus the customer's reading). Download the logger trace when it is returned or via app.

5.18 **Out-of-temperature or damaged delivery:**
- The customer reports it with a photo and reading within the agreed window (e.g. 24 hours).
- Replace or credit it according to the terms (Module 8 pricing guide).
- Record the incident and root cause, such as a courier delay, insufficient coolant or a packing fault.

5.19 Two or more failures on a route or courier trigger a review: change the service, increase the coolant, or change the dispatch day.

5.20 **Seasonal validation:** in summer and winter extremes, run a test shipment with a logger before relying on a new route or packaging design.

## 6. Accountability Checkpoints

| Checkpoint | Signed by | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Courier accepts the product type (live / dry ice) | Dispatch lead | Each new service / route |
| Product temperature within range before packing | Dispatch operator | Each consignment |
| Labels, Batch IDs and documents checked | Dispatch operator | Each consignment |
| Logger started and ID recorded | Dispatch operator | Each logged consignment |
| Receipt temperature and signature obtained | Dispatch lead | Each delivery |
| Incident log reviewed | Farm manager | Monthly |

## 7. Records
Dispatch Log (order, Batch IDs, packing and dispatch temperatures, logger ID, courier, tracking, receipt temperature, signature); logger traces; incident reports. Keep them for at least 2 years.

## 8. Deviations
Record any dispatch made outside this SOP (e.g. no logger on a long route, or a weekend transit), with the reason and the approval.
