# SOP M2: Daily Micro-Climate Logging and Substrate Testing

| SOP No. | Version | Effective date | Review date | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SW-SOP-M2 | 1.0 | [date] | [date + 12 months] | Farm Manager |

## 1. Purpose
To keep every unit within the species' temperature, humidity and substrate targets, and to detect and correct problems before they cause dormancy, disease or death.

## 2. Scope
All production, breeding, nursery and quarantine units, indoor and outdoor.

## 3. Responsibilities
- **Duty operator:** morning and evening rounds (5.1–5.8), weekly checks (5.9–5.10).
- **Farm manager:** reviews the logs weekly, carries out monthly substrate pH testing and calibration (5.11–5.13), and handles escalations (section 6).

## 4. Safety Warnings
> ⚠️ **Electrical safety:** misting systems and heaters combine water and electricity. Check that RCD/GFCI protection is fitted and tested. Never handle wet electrical equipment.
>
> ⚠️ **Heat and humidity:** take breaks and drink water when working in hot, humid rooms.
>
> ⚠️ **Water quality:** use clean, dechlorinated water for misting. Chlorine, salt and chemical residues harm snails.

## 5. Procedure

### Targets (post these in every unit)

| | *Cornu aspersum* | *Achatina* group |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 18–22 °C (acceptable 15–24) | 24–28 °C (acceptable 22–30) |
| Relative humidity | 75–90 % | 80–95 % |
| Substrate pH | 7.0–8.0 | 7.0–8.0 |

### Morning round (within 1 hour of start) and evening round (before dusk)
5.1 **Read the temperature and relative humidity** at stock level in each unit, using the unit's calibrated thermometer and hygrometer or logger display.

5.2 **Record the readings** with the time and your initials in the Daily Micro-Climate Log.

5.3 **Compare them with the targets.** If a reading is out of range, act straight away:

| Problem | Corrective action |
|---|---|
| Humidity low | Pulse misting; wet surfaces (not to standing water); close vents briefly; add shade |
| Humidity high or stagnant | Increase air movement or ventilation; pause misting; remove standing water |
| Temperature high | Shade, ventilate, evaporative cooling; mist (cools by evaporation) |
| Temperature low | Close vents and night curtains; heating on; move nursery boxes to the warmest zone |

5.4 **Recheck after 1 hour** and record the reading and the action taken.

5.5 **Misting routine** (low-tech units): mist morning and evening, and at midday in hot or dry weather (e.g. the Harmattan or dry seasons). Aim for moist surfaces, not puddles. Automated systems: confirm the humidistat setting and that the nozzles are clear.

5.6 **Look at the units:** condensation dripping onto boxes, standing water, dry or crusted substrate, and the **% of snails sealed with an epiphragm**. Estimate and record it.

5.7 **Check escape-proofing:** lids latched, anti-escape wire live and clear of vegetation, doors and seals intact.

5.8 **Substrate moisture squeeze test** (one spot per unit, rotating location). A handful should hold together without dripping. If it's dry, moisten it evenly; if it's wet, improve drainage and ventilation.

### Weekly
5.9 Squeeze-test the substrate in **three locations** per unit.

5.10 **Download the data loggers.** Record the weekly minimum and maximum temperature and humidity per unit, and flag any period out of range.

### Monthly and between batches (farm manager)
5.11 **Substrate pH test** (3 samples per unit):
1. Take ~50 g from the top 5 cm at three points.
2. Mix each sample with distilled water at 1 part substrate to 2 parts water. Stir, and leave for 30 minutes.
3. Read with a calibrated pH meter, or a test kit for low-tech farms.
4. Record the 3 readings and the mean.

5.12 **Prevent acidification:**
- **pH below 7.0:** mix in ground limestone (start with ~50–100 g per m² of bed or per 10 litres of substrate), moisten, and retest after 7 days.
- **pH below 6.5, or a foul smell:** replace the substrate.
- Remove residues daily (SOP M3), because decomposing feed and droppings acidify the substrate.

5.13 **Calibrate the instruments:**
- Hygrometers and thermometers against a reference instrument, quarterly.
- The pH meter with buffer solutions (pH 7 and pH 4 or 10) before each monthly test.
- Record the results.

## 6. Escalation and Accountability Checkpoints

| Trigger | Action | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Out of range for > 24 h | Notify the manager; find the root cause (equipment fault, weather, water supply) | Duty operator → manager |
| > 10 % of a growing unit sealed | Treat as climate stress; review settings; inspect for disease | Manager |
| Alarm (indoor) or power failure | Respond immediately; switch to back-up power | Duty operator |

| Checkpoint | Signed by | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| AM/PM log complete for all units | Duty operator | Daily |
| Weekly logger review | Farm manager | Weekly |
| pH tests and calibration recorded | Farm manager | Monthly |

## 7. Records
Daily Micro-Climate Log; logger downloads; Substrate pH Log; Calibration Log. Keep them for at least 2 years.

## 8. Deviations
Record any missed round or uncorrected out-of-range reading, with its reason and corrective action.
