# Snail Farming in Europe and the Mediterranean

Europe has the world's most established market for edible snails. France alone eats tens of thousands of tonnes a year, with a sharp peak at Christmas. Italy, Spain, Portugal and Greece have deep snail traditions of their own, and the UK supplies a growing fine-dining sector. Much of this demand is still met by imports and by wild collection in central and eastern Europe and the eastern Mediterranean. That leaves room for well-run farms supplying fresh, traceable product.

The region also has the most developed regulation. Producers who understand EU food hygiene, animal by-product and wildlife rules from the start have a real advantage.

> **Legal notice:** This guide is educational and summarises EU and UK frameworks as we understand them. It is not legal advice. National rules add requirements, and retained EU law in Great Britain may diverge over time. Confirm every requirement with your competent authority.

---

## Section 1: Regional Species and Production Framework

### Dominant species

| Species | Trade names | Status |
|---|---|---|
| ***Cornu aspersum aspersum*** (syn. *Helix aspersa*) | Petit-gris | **The main farmed species.** Adaptable, fast-growing, established market |
| ***Cornu aspersum maxima*** | Gros-gris | Larger, farmed strain; popular for indoor systems |
| ***Helix pomatia*** | Burgundy or Roman snail, *escargot de Bourgogne* | **Protected or restricted.** Listed in Annex V of the EU Habitats Directive; protected in Great Britain under Schedule 5 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. Slow-growing and rarely farmed; most supply is regulated wild collection |
| ***Helix lucorum*** | Turkish snail | Mostly wild-collected in Turkey, Greece and the Balkans for export |
| ***Otala*, *Theba*, *Eobania*** species | *Cabrillas*, *caracoles* | Small Mediterranean species; mostly wild-collected, important in Spain and Morocco |

**Recommendation:** farm *Cornu aspersum*, either the petit-gris or the gros-gris strain depending on your system and buyers. Do not collect or farm *Helix pomatia* without confirming its legal status where you are.

### Environmental and housing fit

| System | Description | Typical region |
|---|---|---|
| **Italian outdoor ("Cherasco") system** | Complete cycle in planted, fenced outdoor beds; overwintering in place | Northern and central Italy; mild temperate zones |
| **French mixed system** | Indoor climate-controlled breeding and nursery; outdoor or polytunnel fattening in summer | France, UK, Benelux |
| **Greenhouse and polytunnel** | Protected beds or tables with shade and irrigation | Spain, Portugal, Greece, southern Italy |
| **Intensive indoor racks** | Fully climate-controlled rooms with tiered boxes; year-round output | Northern Europe; high-value or year-round contracts |

**Targets for *Cornu aspersum*:** 18–22 °C for growth (acceptable 15–24 °C), relative humidity 75–90 %. Dormancy sets in below about 5–7 °C, and heat stress and aestivation above about 27 °C.

## Section 2: Agricultural Compliance and Legal Framework

### Wildlife, species and environmental law

- **Habitats Directive (92/43/EEC):** *Helix pomatia* is listed in Annex V, so member states may regulate its collection and exploitation, and many do, with closed seasons, minimum sizes, permits or bans.
- **Great Britain:** releasing non-native animals, or allowing them to escape, is an offence under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, section 14. *Helix pomatia* is protected under Schedule 5.
- **Tropical species:** farming *Achatina*-group snails in Europe is possible only in fully enclosed, heated buildings, and it carries serious legal and biosecurity risks. Take legal advice first.
- **Planning and environmental permits:** polytunnels, buildings, drainage and wash-water discharge may need planning consent and an environmental permit.

### Food hygiene for snails

- **Regulation (EC) No 852/2004:** general food hygiene. Register the food business with the competent authority; in England, register with the local authority at least 28 days before trading.
- **Regulation (EC) No 853/2004, Annex III, Section XI:** the specific rules for snails. Snails must be killed in an establishment, and the hepatopancreas must be removed where it may present a hazard and must not be used for human consumption. Some activities need establishment **approval**, not just registration. Check with your authority.
- **HACCP:** a documented food safety management system covering purging, conditioning, dispatch, evisceration, cooling and storage.
- **Labelling (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011):** molluscs are one of the 14 allergens that must be declared, along with any butter, garlic preparations and so on in prepared products.

### Animal by-products

- **Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009** (and its GB equivalent) covers mortalities, rejected stock, shells and processing waste. They must go through approved routes, and some uses, such as shells in animal feed, are restricted. Agree a compliant disposal and processing route before stocking.

### Import, export and trade

- **Within the EU:** consignments need commercial documents and traceability back to the producer.
- **Imports from outside the EU:** snails for human consumption may enter only from **listed third countries** and **approved establishments**, with official certificates and border control checks. Confirm the current lists before sourcing or selling.
- **GB and EU trade:** since Brexit, product moving between Great Britain and the EU needs certification and border checks. Plan for the extra time and cost.

### Processing standards

- Separate zones for live intake, purging and conditioning, dispatch, evisceration and packing; washable, cleanable surfaces; potable water; temperature-controlled storage (0–5 °C chilled, −18 °C frozen).
- Validated cooking, cooling (to below 8 °C within 90 minutes in UK practice) and pasteurisation processes. Use a licensed thermal process for canned products.
- **Snail caviar:** confirm food-law status, including any novel food considerations, with your authority before launch.
- **Mucin:** cosmetics law applies (Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, or the UK Cosmetics Regulation), including a safety assessment and a Responsible Person for finished products.

## Section 3: Climate Adaptation and Risk Mitigation

### Micro-climate engineering

- **Winter frost (northern and central Europe):** bring breeders indoors for a controlled rest of 6–8 weeks at 5–7 °C, then raise them to 18–20 °C under long-day lighting (16–18 hours) to trigger breeding. Insulate indoor units, and protect outdoor beds with straw mulch and frost fleece if overwintering in place.
- **Summer heat and drought (Mediterranean):** use 50–70 % shade netting over beds, evening and early-morning irrigation, and a mixed cover crop for shade and humidity. Use evaporative cooling in greenhouses. Watch for aestivation above about 27 °C.
- **Humidity control indoors:** pulsed misting linked to a humidistat, continuous gentle air movement, and condensation management.
- **Monitoring:** calibrated loggers at stock level, with alarms for indoor units.

### Water and substrate management

- **Water:** mains or borehole water, checked for chlorine and hardness. Water restrictions in Mediterranean summers make drip and micro-misting systems and rainwater storage essential. Avoid water contaminated with agrochemical run-off.
- **Calcium:** feed-grade limestone and dicalcium phosphate are widely available. Cuttlebone and ground oyster shell make useful free-choice supplements.
- **Substrate:** free-draining, calcium-rich loam at about pH 7–8. Lime acidic soils (common in parts of northern Europe), and avoid waterlogged sites.
- **Feed:** formulated meal (about 15–18 % crude protein, 10–13 % calcium, **no salt**) plus a cover crop of rape, radish, clover and chard.

## Section 4: Local Market Access and Economic Channels

### Primary buyers

| Channel | What they buy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fine-dining restaurants** | Fresh or frozen cleaned meat, prepared escargots, shells for service, caviar | Value provenance, consistency and size grading |
| **Specialist processors** (especially in France) | Live or frozen snails in volume | Lower price; contract volumes; strong seasonal peak before Christmas |
| **Delicatessens and farm shops** | Prepared escargots (e.g. *à la bourguignonne*), jars, caviar | Higher margin; branding and labelling matter |
| **Markets and festivals** | Live or cooked snails | Strong in Spain (e.g. Lleida's *Aplec del Caragol*), Italy and Portugal |
| **Direct-to-consumer online** | Frozen prepared products, gift boxes, caviar | Needs cold-chain delivery |
| **Cosmetic laboratories** | Snail secretion filtrate | Batch documentation and testing |

### Pricing, grading and economic viability

**Grading standard:**

| Grade | *Cornu aspersum* live weight | Shell |
|---|---|---|
| Petit-gris standard | ~7–12 g | Fully formed, hard lip; intact |
| Gros-gris standard | ~15–25 g | Fully formed, hard lip; intact |

Reject snails with a soft or incomplete lip, cracked shells or poor retraction.

**Economics:**

- **Seasonality:** the December price peak in France and wider Europe rewards farms that can hold or freeze stock for the season.
- **Value addition:** prepared escargots, caviar and shell sales lift revenue per square metre. Imported wild-collected snails compete on price, so compete on freshness, traceability and quality.
- **Break-even price per kg** = (fixed costs + variable costs per cycle) ÷ saleable kg. Energy is the largest cost in intensive indoor units, so model it carefully.

**Cold chain:** live *Cornu* should be transported dormant, cool (about 4–10 °C) and ventilated. Processed product needs 0–5 °C chilled or −18 °C frozen. Use refrigerated carriers for cross-border shipments, and include temperature records in the documentation.

**Sustainability:** farming relieves pressure on protected wild species such as *Helix pomatia*, and gives restaurants and retailers a traceable, low-footprint protein.
